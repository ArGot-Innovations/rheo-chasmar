# NEXUS CONTEXT

HTLA represents heterogeneous labeled state:

    H = direct-sum_l R^(n_l)

Aligned-label similarity may use a weighted inner product:

    <H,K>_w
        =
    sum_l w_l <H_l,K_l>

Under appropriate strictly positive weights this induces a norm and therefore a standard cosine-type geometry.

Cross-label comparison may instead use something like:

    C_W(H,K)
        =
    sum_(l,m)
        w_lm
        <P_(l->m) H_l, K_m>

where:

- P_(l->m) is an explicitly configured cross-label transform;
- w_lm may be asymmetric;
- the resulting global operator need not be symmetric;
- it need not be positive semidefinite;
- it need not induce a norm;
- it need not define a distance;
- it need not satisfy triangle inequality.

Therefore:

    cross-label compatibility score
        !=
    inner product

and:

    compatibility score
        !=
    metric

unless the required mathematical properties are independently established.

The research must determine what this means for ANN indexing.

## 2. Distinguish the relevant geometric structures

Give rigorous definitions and relationships among at least:

### Metric

d(x,y) >= 0

d(x,y) = 0 iff x = y

d(x,y) = d(y,x)

d(x,z) <= d(x,y) + d(y,z)

### Pseudometric

Allows:

    d(x,y)=0

for distinct x and y.

### Semimetric

Research the terminology carefully because literature varies.

Do not assume one universal definition.

### Quasimetric / quasi-metric

Investigate asymmetric distance functions.

For example:

    d(x,y) != d(y,x)

while some directed triangle condition may still hold.

Determine the standard terminology in the relevant mathematical and ANN literature.

### Similarity

A larger-is-better score that may not define a distance.

### Kernel

Determine when:

    K(x,y)

is positive semidefinite and therefore corresponds to an inner product in a feature space.

### Indefinite kernel / similarity

Investigate what fails when PSD does not hold.

### Divergence

Examples such as KL divergence:

    D_KL(p || q)

which is asymmetric and does not satisfy ordinary metric axioms.

Investigate how nearest-neighbor search is handled for such quantities.

# 3. WHICH TRIANGLE PROPERTY, IF ANY, DOES NEXUS NEED?

This is the central research problem.

Investigate at least:

## Ordinary triangle inequality

    d(x,z)
        <=
    d(x,y) + d(y,z)

## Relaxed triangle inequalities

For some constant c >= 1:

    d(x,z)
        <=
    c [d(x,y) + d(y,z)]

Determine whether such spaces appear in ANN research and whether guarantees survive.

## Directed / quasimetric triangle inequality

For asymmetric d:

    d(x,z)
        <=
    d(x,y) + d(y,z)

while:

    d(x,y)
        !=
    d(y,x)

Investigate whether this is sufficient for useful directed retrieval structures.

## Four-point / negative-type properties

Investigate whether embeddings or ANN methods rely more naturally on:

- negative type;
- Euclidean embeddability;
- doubling dimension;
- bounded expansion;
- intrinsic dimensionality;
- low-dimensional manifold assumptions;

rather than triangle inequality alone.

## Monotonic ordering

Could HNSW require only that a supplied score preserve a meaningful ranking?

For a similarity s and distance d, if:

    s(x,y) = f(d(x,y))

for strictly monotone f,

then ranking is preserved.

Investigate when this is sufficient.
