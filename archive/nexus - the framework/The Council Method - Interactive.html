The Council Method

Four seats.
One conversation.

Most people ask one AI response to do four different jobs at once. That's why it comes back generic.

Pick what you're actually doing right now. Get the exact thing to paste into whatever chat window you already have open.

Seat A · The Messy Start

Shaping a messy idea

Don't clean the idea up before you say it. A clarifying question coming back isn't friction — that's the actual process starting.

The tell: if the answer feels like it could've been written before you said anything specific, you haven't given it enough yet.

Seat B · The Constraint Check

Getting it right the first time

Ban solutions until the constraints exist. Spend the first stretch of the conversation only naming what's true, before anything gets proposed.

The tell: you're saying "not yet, tell me X first" more often than "no, try again."

Paste this, then name the actual task

I want help with [describe the task]. Don't propose solutions yet. First, ask me about the constraints that actually matter — budget, audience, format, timeline, what "good" looks like, anything else you'd need to know to avoid guessing. Keep asking until you actually have enough. Only then give me a real answer.

Seat C · The Pressure Test

Stress-testing something real

Assign it an explicit adversarial job, not a validating one. Force a split between what's a fact and what's just a convention you chose.

The tell: if it agrees fast, that's a reason to push harder, not a reason to relax.

I'm sharing something I've already built or decided on. Don't be encouraging — find where this actually breaks. Tell me which parts are verified facts and which are just conventions or assumptions I've made. Give me the specific failure point, not a general opinion, and say how confident you are that it's a real problem versus just a preference. Here it is: [paste your thing here]

Seat D · The Loose Take

Thinking out loud, on purpose

Say the dumb version first, before you've decided if it's worth saying. Most good material dies in the half-second before you type it.

The tell: if you're slightly embarrassed by what you just said, you probably just said something real.
