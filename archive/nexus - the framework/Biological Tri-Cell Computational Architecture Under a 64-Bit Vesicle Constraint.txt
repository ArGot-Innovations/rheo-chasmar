Biological Tri-Cell Computational Architecture Under a 64-Bit Vesicle Constraint
Biological grounding and design commitments
This specification defines an in silico multicellular tissue of cells (target ) in which each cell is a tripartite computational unit: Nucleus (identity + regulation), Cytoplasm (processing + prediction + metabolism), and Membrane (receptors + interface + vesicle emission/uptake). The tissue achieves (i) multi-scale consensus (“quorum” decisions emerging from local chemical context), (ii) lesion-resilient regeneration after sudden necrosis, and (iii) local Lyapunov-style homeostatic control, under a strict communication and energy budget.
This work uses biological primitives as the implementation vocabulary: vesicles (EV-like carriers), receptor binding and uptake, transcriptional regulation, signal degradation, ATP limits, mitosis, apoptosis, adhesion, and diffusion-like spread through a tissue adjacency lattice. The communication bottleneck is enforced as: each living cell emits exactly one 64-bit-equivalent vesicle payload per its own biological cycle, and each cell can internalize at most 8 vesicle payloads per its own cycle.
The implementation is biologically inspired by extracellular vesicles (EVs), including exosomes (commonly described as ~30–150 nm vesicles) and more broadly “small EVs,” which are widely discussed as intercellular carriers of proteins and nucleic acids (including RNAs) and can be internalized through multiple uptake routes (endocytosis variants, phagocytosis/macropinocytosis, and fusion-like mechanisms).
For tissue-scale coordination, the chemical field behavior is grounded in reaction–diffusion thinking: spatial patterns can arise from local reactions plus diffusion-like spread, with classic analysis showing symmetry breaking and pattern emergence from initially homogeneous states. For population-wide switching, the “quorum” mechanism is taken from canonical bacterial quorum sensing: cells release chemical signals whose extracellular accumulation tracks local density and triggers coordinated gene-expression changes when surpassing thresholds.

Mitosis (regrowth). A living cell triggers mitosis when: - density cue (space is available), and - exceeds a threshold, and - ATP exceeds a mitosis cost.
Mitosis selects a neighboring empty site and instantiates a daughter cell with inherited nuclear identity , TFs partially copied (with noise), and reset membrane receptors. Contact-inhibition is captured by the density gating (mitosis is suppressed when local density is high), consistent with models that use logistic-like growth as a caricature for contact inhibition effects in cell populations.
Apoptosis (stability enforcement). Apoptosis triggers if stress exceeds for sustained time or if nuclear apoptosis factor is high. This prevents runaway accumulation of dysfunctional cells (a key anti-pathology operator).

Intuition: - the first term enforces local consensus, - the second penalizes accumulated stress, - the third penalizes energy deficit (cannot maintain homeostasis), - the fourth penalizes low density (lesion boundary pressure).
A cell-aware control heuristic then adjusts transcription/emission to reduce : - If dominates, bias emission toward “homeostasis” vesicle types that carry more precisely (higher quantization priority). - If density deficit dominates, bias emission toward “repair/mitosis” flags and increase and . - If stress dominates, increase apoptosis vigilance unless density is low (to avoid self-elimination at wound edge).
This is a Lyapunov-style regulator because it is explicitly built to reduce a nonnegative “energy” via local actions. (The simulator logs both and aggregate tissue proxies; Section “Experimental protocols.”)
Vesicle payload protocol as a 32-nt RNA string
Why 32 nucleotides implement a 64-bit constraint
A 4-letter nucleotide alphabet can encode up to bits per nucleotide in principle; practical DNA/ RNA data storage often achieves somewhat less than 2 bits/nt due to coding constraints (e.g., avoiding problematic motifs), but the 2-bit/nt bound provides the clean 64-bit-equivalent mapping target used here.
Separately, EVs are widely documented to carry RNAs (including mRNAs and noncoding RNAs), motivating an RNA-sequence substrate as a plausible symbolic stand-in for an EV cargo channel in the simulator.

Packing, release, uptake, and decay rules
Vesicle formation (membrane emission). - Exactly one vesicle is formed per cell cycle boundary for each alive cell. - A fixed ATP cost is charged for “bud + load + release.” The simulator enforces this energy cost and can force a cell into failure if mandatory emission is unaffordable.
Uptake and decoding. - Uptake uses the receptor-gated probabilistic mechanism described earlier. -Decoding runs only inside the recipient cytoplasm after internalization; payload is discarded after decoding. −“Competitivebinding”ishandledby(i)receptoravailability,and(ii)uptakecapacity≤8percellcycle;vesicles beyond capacity degrade (collision handling at the membrane capacity boundary). Uptake diversity and binding/uptake mechanisms are abstracted from EV literature into this capacity-limited stochastic gate.
Signal degradation. - A vesicle can be lost before uptake via TTL expiry or per-step degradation probability tied to (extracellular clearance), reflecting that EVs can be cleared/degraded and that uptake is not guaranteed.

Allowed and forbidden operations (anti-nonphysical guardrails)
This simulator enforces: - Allowed: local receptor binding/uptake, local TF updates, local vesicle emission, diffusion-like hops to adjacent adhered neighbors, stochastic decay, mitosis into adjacent empty sites, apoptosis of individual cells. - Forbidden: instant long-range signaling, global averaging in the cell logic, broadcasting to all cells, unlimited vesicle reception, emitting >1 vesicle per cycle, and energy-free transcription/receptor turnover.
These guardrails ensure the tissue’s coordination cannot “cheat” by using omniscient global state.
Metabolic budget model (ATP as compute limit)
We assign each cell a per-cycle ATP production plus a bounded carryover. ATP is consumed by: -vesicle emission (mandatory cost), - processing each internalized vesicle (decoder cost), - TF updates (regulatory cost), - receptor turnover (membrane maintenance), - mitosis (large cost), - motility/adhesion change (optional cost).
The high cost of protein synthesis is commonly discussed as ~4 ATP-equivalents per peptide bond (ATP/ GTP equivalents), which motivates assigning large costs to growth (mitosis) relative to small signaling steps.
These costs are not intended to be quantitatively exact cell biophysics; they are a strict accounting mechanism to enforce the design constraint that “computation” is metabolically limited.

# -----------------------------
# Utilities: CRC-12 (simple, reproducible)
# -----------------------------
CRC12_POLY = 0x80F # CRC-12/UMTS polynomial (common 12-bit CRC poly)
def crc12(bits: List[int]) -> int:
"""Compute CRC-12 over a list of bits (0/1). Returns 12-bit int."""
reg = 0
for b in bits:
reg = ((reg << 1) | (b & 1)) & 0x1FFF # keep 13 bits for division step
if reg & 0x1000: # if top bit set
reg ^= CRC12_POLY
return reg & 0xFFF
def int_to_bits(x: int, width: int) -> List[int]:
return [(x >> (width - 1 - i)) & 1 for i in range(width)]
def bits_to_int(bits: List[int]) -> int:
x = 0
for b in bits:
x = (x << 1) | (b & 1)
return x
RNA_MAP = {0b00: "A", 0b01: "C", 0b10: "G", 0b11: "U"}
RNA_INV = {"A": 0b00, "C": 0b01, "G": 0b10, "U": 0b11}
def bits_to_rna(bits64: List[int]) -> str:
assert len(bits64) == 64
out = []
for i in range(0, 64, 2):
two = (bits64[i] << 1) | bits64[i + 1]
out.append(RNA_MAP[two])
return "".join(out)
def rna_to_bits(rna32: str) -> List[int]:
assert len(rna32) == 32
bits = []
for ch in rna32:
v = RNA_INV[ch]
bits.append((v >> 1) & 1)
bits.append(v & 1)
assert len(bits) == 64
return bits
def q_clip(x: float, max_int: int) -> int:
return max(0, min(max_int, int(round(x))))
def q12(x: float, max_val: float = 1.0) -> int:
# map [0, max_val] to [0, 4095]
return q_clip((x / max_val) * 4095.0, 4095)
def uq12(u: int, max_val: float = 1.0) -> float:
return (u / 4095.0) * max_val
def q8(x: float, max_val: float = 1.0) -> int:
return q_clip((x / max_val) * 255.0, 255)
def uq8(u: int, max_val: float = 1.0) -> float:
return (u / 255.0) * max_val

def _moore_neighbors(self, idx: int) -> List[int]:
x = idx % self.w
y = idx // self.w
nn = []
for dy in (-1, 0, 1):
for dx in (-1, 0, 1):
if dx == 0 and dy == 0:
continue
xx = x + dx
yy = y + dy
if 0 <= xx < self.w and 0 <= yy < self.h:
nn.append(yy * self.w + xx)
return nn
def _alive_neighbor_density(self, idx: int) -> float:
nbrs = self.neighbors[idx]
if not nbrs:
return 0.0
alive = sum(1 for j in nbrs if self.cells[j].alive)
return alive / float(len(nbrs))

# Payload pack/unpack
# -----------------------------
def pack_payload(self, cell: Cell, message_type: int, flags: int) ->
Tuple[int, int]:
preamble = 0b1010 # 4-bit
identity = cell.nucleus.identity & 0xFF
k = message_type & 0xF
s_q = q12(cell.cytoplasm.s_hat, max_val=1.0)
u_q = q12(min(1.0, cell.cytoplasm.stress / 2.0), max_val=1.0) #
normalize stress ~[0,2] -> [0,1]
rho_q = q8(cell.cytoplasm.density_local, max_val=1.0)
flags4 = flags & 0xF
bits = []
bits += int_to_bits(preamble, 4)
bits += int_to_bits(identity, 8)
bits += int_to_bits(k, 4)
bits += int_to_bits(s_q, 12)
bits += int_to_bits(u_q, 12)
bits += int_to_bits(rho_q, 8)
bits += int_to_bits(flags4, 4)
c = crc12(bits)
bits += int_to_bits(c, 12)
assert len(bits) == 64
x = bits_to_int(bits)
hi = (x >> 32) & 0xFFFFFFFF
lo = x & 0xFFFFFFFF
return hi, lo
def unpack_payload(self, ves: Vesicle) -> Optional[Dict[str, int]]:
bits = ves.as_uint64_bits()
head = bits[:52]
given_crc = bits_to_int(bits[52:])
if crc12(head) != given_crc:
return None
preamble = bits_to_int(bits[0:4])
if preamble != 0b1010:
return None
identity = bits_to_int(bits[4:12])
k = bits_to_int(bits[12:16])
s_q = bits_to_int(bits[16:28])
u_q = bits_to_int(bits[28:40])
rho_q = bits_to_int(bits[40:48])
flags = bits_to_int(bits[48:52])
return {"identity": identity, "k": k, "s_q": s_q, "u_q": u_q, "rho_q":
rho_q, "flags": flags}

# Vesicle ecology: hop, decay, uptake
# -----------------------------
def _attempt_uptake(self, target_idx: int, ves: Vesicle) -> bool:
cell = self.cells[target_idx]
if not cell.alive:
return False
if cell.membrane.uptake_count_cycle >= self.p.max_uptake_per_cycle:
return False
# receptor-gated uptake probability (abstracted)
R = max(0.0, cell.membrane.receptors[ves.message_type])
p_bind = 1.0 - math.exp(-self.p.kon * R * self.p.dt)
if random.random() < p_bind:
# internalize
cell.membrane.uptake_pool.append(ves)
return True
return False
def _vesicle_step(self):
new_ves = []
for ves in self.vesicles:
if ves.ttl <= 0:
continue
if random.random() < self.p.decay_p:
continue
# hop along adhesion neighborhood
cur = ves.carrier
nbrs = self.neighbors[cur]
if nbrs:
nxt = random.choice(nbrs)
ves.carrier = nxt
ves.ttl -= 1
# attempt uptake at current location
if self._attempt_uptake(ves.carrier, ves):
continue # consumed
new_ves.append(ves)
self.vesicles = new_ves

# reset cycle counters
cell.membrane.uptake_count_cycle = 0
# ATP production
cell.cytoplasm.atp = min(p.atp_max, cell.cytoplasm.atp +
p.atp_prod_per_cycle)
# (1) Membrane uptake processing: up to 8
s_in = []
u_in = []
rho_in = []
flags_in = []
take = min(p.max_uptake_per_cycle, len(cell.membrane.uptake_pool))
for _ in range(take):
ves = cell.membrane.uptake_pool.pop(0)
decoded = self.unpack_payload(ves)
cell.membrane.uptake_count_cycle += 1
cell.cytoplasm.atp -= p.cost_decode_per_ves
if decoded is None:
continue
s_in.append(uq12(decoded["s_q"], max_val=1.0))
u_in.append(uq12(decoded["u_q"], max_val=1.0))
rho_in.append(uq8(decoded["rho_q"], max_val=1.0))
flags_in.append(decoded["flags"])
# estimate density locally from alive neighbors (not from any global
signal)
cell.cytoplasm.density_local = self._alive_neighbor_density(cell.idx)
if s_in:
s_bar = sum(s_in) / len(s_in)
else:
s_bar = cell.cytoplasm.s_hat # no new info
# (2) Cytoplasm update (consensus + stress)
# noise at integration stage
noise = random.gauss(0.0, p.sigma_e)
s_new = (1.0 - p.alpha_consensus) * cell.cytoplasm.s_hat +
p.alpha_consensus * s_bar
cell.cytoplasm.s_hat = max(0.0, min(1.0, s_new + noise))
mismatch = abs(cell.cytoplasm.s_hat - s_bar)
density_def = max(0.0, p.density_target - cell.cytoplasm.density_local)
cell.cytoplasm.stress = max(
0.0,
(1.0 - p.stress_decay) * cell.cytoplasm.stress
+ p.stress_gain_mismatch * mismatch
+ p.stress_gain_density * density_def
)
# Basic phi-proxy: alive fraction is computed globally for logging only;
# cells don't have access to it. Locally, use coherence proxy:
nbrs = [j for j in self.neighbors[cell.idx] if self.cells[j].alive]
if nbrs:
d = sum(abs(cell.cytoplasm.s_hat - self.cells[j].cytoplasm.s_hat)
for j in nbrs) / len(nbrs)
coherence = math.exp(-3.0 * d)
else:
coherence = 0.0
cell.cytoplasm.phi_proxy = coherence
# (3) Nucleus regulation
# repair and mitosis increase with stress/density deficit; apoptosis
increases with sustained high stress
cell.cytoplasm.atp -= p.cost_regulate
cell.nucleus.tf_repair = min(1.0, 0.85 * cell.nucleus.tf_repair + 0.30
* min(1.0, cell.cytoplasm.stress))
cell.nucleus.tf_mit = min(1.0, 0.85 * cell.nucleus.tf_mit + 0.60 *
min(1.0, density_def))
cell.nucleus.tf_apo = min(1.0, 0.90 * cell.nucleus.tf_apo + 0.40 *
max(0.0, cell.cytoplasm.stress - 0.7))
cell.nucleus.tf_homeo = max(0.0, min(1.0, 0.95 * cell.nucleus.tf_homeo
+ 0.05 * (1.0 - mismatch)))
# (4) Fate decisions
# apoptosis
if cell.cytoplasm.stress > p.stress_apoptosis and cell.nucleus.tf_apo >
0.7:
cell.alive = False
return
# mitosis: revive a neighboring dead site
if cell.nucleus.tf_mit > p.mitosis_thresh and cell.cytoplasm.atp >=
p.cost_mitosis:
dead_sites = [j for j in self.neighbors[cell.idx] if not
self.cells[j].alive]
if dead_sites:
j = random.choice(dead_sites)
self._revive_cell(parent=cell, target=self.cells[j])
cell.cytoplasm.atp -= p.cost_mitosis
# optional motility: move into adjacent dead site (swap occupancy
conceptually)
if cell.nucleus.tf_repair > 0.6 and cell.cytoplasm.atp >= p.cost_move:
dead_sites = [j for j in self.neighbors[cell.idx] if not
self.cells[j].alive]
if dead_sites and random.random() < 0.15:
j = random.choice(dead_sites)
# "move" by reviving target and killing self (a crude migration
proxy)
self._revive_cell(parent=cell, target=self.cells[j])
cell.alive = False
return
# (5) Membrane receptor expression (simple coupling)
for k in range(16):
base = 20.0
bump = 30.0 * (cell.nucleus.tf_homeo if k == 1 else 0.0)
bump += 25.0 * (cell.nucleus.tf_repair if k == 2 else 0.0)
bump += 25.0 * (cell.nucleus.tf_mit if k == 3 else 0.0)
bump += 20.0 * (cell.nucleus.tf_apo if k == 4 else 0.0)
cell.membrane.receptors[k] = max(0.0, min(100.0, 0.9 *
cell.membrane.receptors[k] + 0.1 * (base + bump)))
# (6) Vesicle emission: exactly one per cycle
# choose message type by dominant TF state
message_type = 1
flags = 0
if cell.nucleus.tf_repair > 0.5:
message_type = 2
flags |= 0b0001
if cell.nucleus.tf_mit > 0.6 and cell.cytoplasm.density_local <
p.density_target:
message_type = 3
flags |= 0b0010
if cell.nucleus.tf_apo > 0.7:
message_type = 4
flags |= 0b0100
# mandatory emission ATP cost
cell.cytoplasm.atp -= p.cost_emit
if cell.cytoplasm.atp < 0.0:
# energy failure => necrotic
cell.alive = False
return
hi, lo = self.pack_payload(cell, message_type=message_type, flags=flags)
ves = Vesicle(
payload_hi=hi,
payload_lo=lo,
message_type=message_type,
carrier=cell.idx,
ttl=p.vesicle_ttl
)
self.vesicles.append(ves)
def _revive_cell(self, parent: Cell, target: Cell):
# create daughter cell in target slot
target.alive = True
target.phase = random.random()
target.rate = random.uniform(0.95, 1.05)
target.nucleus.identity = parent.nucleus.identity
# TF inheritance with mild noise
target.nucleus.tf_homeo = max(0.0, min(1.0, parent.nucleus.tf_homeo +
random.gauss(0.0, 0.03)))
target.nucleus.tf_repair = max(0.0, min(1.0, parent.nucleus.tf_repair +
random.gauss(0.0, 0.03)))
target.nucleus.tf_mit = max(0.0, min(1.0, parent.nucleus.tf_mit +
random.gauss(0.0, 0.03)))
target.nucleus.tf_apo = max(0.0, min(1.0, parent.nucleus.tf_apo +
random.gauss(0.0, 0.03)))
# cytoplasm reset-ish
target.cytoplasm.atp = random.uniform(500.0, 1200.0)
target.cytoplasm.s_hat = parent.cytoplasm.s_hat
target.cytoplasm.stress = 0.0
target.cytoplasm.phi_proxy = 1.0
target.membrane.uptake_pool.clear()
target.membrane.uptake_count_cycle = 0
def apply_lesion(self, frac: float = 0.30, clustered: bool = False):
idxs = [c.idx for c in self.cells if c.alive]
kill = int(frac * len(idxs))
if kill <= 0:
return
if not clustered:
victims = set(random.sample(idxs, kill))
else:
# cluster around a random center
center = random.choice(idxs)
cx, cy = self.cells[center].pos
# kill closest by Manhattan distance
dlist = sorted(idxs, key=lambda i: abs(self.cells[i].pos[0] - cx) +
abs(self.cells[i].pos[1] - cy))
victims = set(dlist[:kill])
for i in victims:
self.cells[i].alive = False
self.cells[i].membrane.uptake_pool.clear()
def step(self):
# vesicle diffusion/uptake substeps
for _ in range(self.p.diffusion_hops_per_dt):
if self.vesicles:
self._vesicle_step()
# advance phases; run local cycle updates when phase crosses 1
for cell in self.cells:
if not cell.alive:
continue
cell.phase += cell.rate * self.p.dt
while cell.phase >= 1.0 and cell.alive:
cell.phase -= 1.0
self._cell_cycle_update(cell)
def run(self, lesion: bool = True, clustered_lesion: bool = False):
# define macro-steps to approximate "global cycles" for observer logging
macro_steps = int(self.p.cycles_total / self.p.dt)
lesion_step = int(self.p.lesion_cycle / self.p.dt)
for t in range(macro_steps + 1):
if lesion and t == lesion_step:
self.apply_lesion(frac=0.30, clustered=clustered_lesion)
self.step()
# observer telemetry (cells do not get access)
if t % int(1.0 / self.p.dt) == 0: # about once per nominal cycle
global_cycle = int(t * self.p.dt)
alive = sum(1 for c in self.cells if c.alive)
mean_phi = sum(c.cytoplasm.phi_proxy for c in self.cells if
c.alive) / max(1, alive)
mean_stress = sum(c.cytoplasm.stress for c in self.cells if
c.alive) / max(1, alive)
self.telemetry.append(TelemetryRow(
global_cycle=global_cycle,
alive=alive,
phi_proxy=mean_phi,
mean_stress=mean_stress,
vesicles_in_space=len(self.vesicles),
))

Failure modes and pathology analyses
This architecture is intentionally capable of both recovery and pathological regimes; the value of the simulator is that these regimes are explicit and testable.
Communication pathologies (membrane-centered). - Receptor downregulation / binding disabled: vesicles rarely internalize neighborhood coupling collapses consensus coherence decays and lesion recovery fails (no coordinated repair cues). EV uptake is known to involve multiple pathways and can be sensitive to receptor/interaction context; this motivates explicitly testing uptake failure. - Uptake saturation / autoimmune-like cascades: if vesicle density is too high (TTL too long, diffusion too strong), high arrival rates force constant overflow beyond the 8-per-cycle cap, increasing information loss and destabilizing control (cells respond to noisy partial views). - Checksum rejection dominance: if CRC failures are artificially increased (ablation), coherent coordination collapses even if diffusion is strong.
Regulation pathologies (nucleus-centered). - Runaway proliferation (cancer-like): removing ATP constraints or making mitosis too cheap yields unchecked mitosis after a lesion, overshooting density and causing instability—illustrating why growth must be metabolically expensive relative to signaling. -Senescence-like trap: if mitosis threshold is too strict or ATP production too low, lesioned regions remain under-filled; boundary cells persist in high stress without sufficient repair action. - Apoptosis cascade: if apoptosis thresholds are too low, post-lesion stress triggers widespread cell death beyond the initial 30% deletion.
