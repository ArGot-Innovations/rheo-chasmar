## PART III — Partial-Unbind Recoverability

### Formal Analysis

For coordinate-separable binding with:
```
bind(q_i, r_i) = 0                    if q_i = 0 or r_i = 0
bind(q_i, r_i) = sign(q_i r_i) × min(|q_i|, |r_i|)  otherwise
```

with `q_i, r_i ∈ {-2, -1, 0, +1, +2}`:

**The proposition is TRUE**: Partial-unbind ambiguity at coordinate `i` is independent of global occupancy `K`.

For a fixed coordinate `i`, given `c_i` and `r_i`:
```
Poss(c_i, r_i) = {q_i ∈ Q : bind(q_i, r_i) = c_i}
```

This depends only on:
- `c_i` (the observed composite value),
- `r_i` (the known binding key value),
- the alphabet `{-2,-1,0,+1,+2}`,
- the binding rule.

**It does not depend on** how many other coordinates are active elsewhere in the vector.

### Table of Poss(c, r)

For each possible `(c, r)` pair:

| c | r | Possible q | |Poss| |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | {-2,-1,0,+1,+2} | 5 |
| 0 | ±1 | {0} | 1 |
| 0 | ±2 | {0} | 1 |
| ±1 | 0 | {-2,-1,0,+1,+2} | 5 |
| ±1 | ±1 | {±1, ±2} | 2 |
| ±1 | ∓1 | ∅ | 0 |
| ±1 | ±2 | ∅ | 0 |
| ±1 | ∓2 | {±1, ±2} | 2 |
| ±2 | 0 | {-2,-1,0,+1,+2} | 5 |
| ±2 | ±1 | {±2} | 1 |
| ±2 | ∓1 | ∅ | 0 |
| ±2 | ±2 | {±1, ±2} | 2 |
| ±2 | ∓2 | ∅ | 0 |

**Key observations:**
- `|Poss| = 0` → **IMPOSSIBLE** (when magnitudes conflict: |c| < |r|)
- `|Poss| = 1` → **EXACT** (when |c| = |r|, or c=0 with r≠0)
- `|Poss| > 1` → **AMBIGUOUS** (when |c| > |r|, or r=0 with c≠0, or both zero)

### Critical Conclusion

**UNKNOWN does not arise from this construction.** The four-state system (EXACT, AMBIGUOUS, UNKNOWN, IMPOSSIBLE) cannot be derived from the coordinate-level partial-unbinding operation alone. If UNKNOWN exists in the system, it must originate from a different mechanism—likely at the whole-vector level (e.g., cleanup memory failure, insufficient overlap for retrieval, or resonator non-convergence).

**Therefore: Claims that `K > 307` causes partial-unbind recoverability to fail are mathematically unsupported** by the coordinate-level binding rule alone.

- The relationship is monotonic (more coordinates = lower collision probability)

**307 does not correspond to any collision probability threshold** in this analysis.

## Mathematical capacity

### Raw quinary state space

For quinary coordinates

\[
\mathcal Q=\{-2,-1,0,+1,+2\},
\]

with exactly \(K\) active, nonzero coordinates, and four choices at each active coordinate, the number of vectors is

\[
N(D,K)=\binom{D}{K}4^K.
\]

For a cap of at most \(K_{\text{wall}}\),

\[
N_{\le K}(D,K_{\text{wall}})
=\sum_{k=0}^{K_{\text{wall}}}\binom{D}{k}4^k.
\]

At \(D=1024\), \(K=307\), direct computation gives:

\[
\log_2 N(1024,307)\approx1510.997\ \text{bits},
\]

and

\[
\log_2 N_{\le307}(1024,307)\approx1511.160\ \text{bits}.
\]

By comparison, the unrestricted quinary space has

\[
5^{1024}
\]

possible vectors, with

\[
\log_2(5^{1024})
=1024\log_2 5
\approx2377.654\ \text{bits}.
\]

Thus the exact-\(307\) sparse subspace retains roughly:

\[
\frac{1511.0}{2377.7}\approx63.5\%
\]

of the unrestricted coordinatewise quinary information capacity, while imposing a substantial occupancy constraint.

That calculation is a **state-count result only**. It says that the constrained representation remains astronomically large. It does not prove anything about semantic separability, collision resistance under a particular operation, cleanup-memory reliability, nearest-neighbor retrieval, or partial-unbinding quality.

### Is 307 a capacity extremum?

For exact \(K\)-sparse quinary vectors,

\[
\log N(D,K)
=
\log \binom{D}{K}+K\log4.
\]

The ratio between adjacent terms is

\[
\frac{N(D,K+1)}{N(D,K)}
=
4\frac{D-K}{K+1}.
\]

The state count continues increasing while

\[
4(D-K)>K+1,
\]

which is approximately true until

\[
K<\frac{4D-1}{5}.
\]

For \(D=1024\), the mode lies near

\[
K\approx819.
\]

Therefore, \(K=307\) is not a state-space maximum, transition, or combinatorial extremum of the raw quinary model. It is on the rising part of the capacity curve: increasing \(K\) substantially beyond 307 still increases the number of representable vectors.

This falsifies one possible post-hoc explanation: **307 is not singled out by raw k-sparse quinary combinatorics.**

## Support overlap

### Exact model

Let \(A,B\subseteq\{1,\ldots,D\}\) be independently selected supports of size \(K\). Their overlap

\[
X=|A\cap B|
\]

has the hypergeometric distribution:

\[
\Pr(X=x)
=
\frac{\binom Kx\binom{D-K}{K-x}}
{\binom DK},
\]

for feasible values of \(x\).

The expectation is

\[
\mathbb E[X]=\frac{K^2}{D}.
\]

The variance is

\[
\operatorname{Var}(X)
=
K\left(\frac KD\right)
\left(1-\frac KD\right)
\left(\frac{D-K}{D-1}\right).
\]

At \(D=1024\), \(K=307\):

\[
\mathbb E[X]
=
\frac{307^2}{1024}
=
92.0400390625,
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{SD}(X)\approx6.72.
\]

So two independently sampled supports at the reference density share about **92 active coordinates on average**, with most overlap values roughly in the neighborhood of 79–105. That is not “strong support separation” in the sense of nearly disjoint supports.

### Comparison across occupancies

| \(K\) | Occupancy \(K/D\) | Expected shared active coordinates \(K^2/D\) | SD of overlap |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 32 | 3.125% | 1 | 0.97 |
| 64 | 6.25% | 4 | 1.88 |
| 128 | 12.5% | 16 | 3.50 |
| 256 | 25.0% | 64 | 6.00 |
| 307 | 29.98% | 92.04 | 6.72 |
| 384 | 37.5% | 144 | 7.50 |
| 512 | 50.0% | 256 | 8.00 |
| 768 | 75.0% | 576 | 6.00 |
| 1024 | 100% | 1024 | 0 |

If nonzero signs are independent and symmetric, an overlapped coordinate has:

- sign agreement probability \(1/2\);
- sign disagreement probability \(1/2\);
- magnitude agreement probability \(1/2\) if magnitudes \(1\) and \(2\) are equiprobable;
- exact nonzero symbol agreement probability \(1/4\).

At \(K=307\), the expected number of same-support coordinates with exact equal nonzero symbols is therefore approximately

\[
\frac{92.04}{4}\approx23.01.
\]

The expected number with equal sign is about

\[
\frac{92.04}{2}\approx46.02.
\]

These values are model-dependent: they assume uniformly sampled supports, independent vectors, balanced signs, and equiprobable magnitudes. Real Nexus-generated vectors could depart materially from all of those assumptions.

### Interpretation

The relationship with occupancy is smooth:

\[
\mathbb E[X]=Dp^2,\qquad p=\frac KD.
\]

There is no discontinuity at \(p\approx0.30\). Increasing \(p\) increases expected overlap quadratically, but that alone does not say whether system-level behavior improves or worsens. Higher \(K\) can provide more redundant signal and higher raw coding entropy; lower \(K\) can lower accidental support overlap and make sparse computation/storage possible. The optimal point depends on the actual decoder, similarity metric, noise source, retrieval workload, and downstream transformations.

## Binding and partial unbinding

### Coordinate-separable binding proposition

The specified quinary binding rule is:

\[
b(q,r)=
\begin{cases}
0,&q=0\ \text{or}\ r=0,\\
\operatorname{sgn}(qr)\min(|q|,|r|),&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

For vectors \(q,r\in\mathcal Q^D\), if binding is coordinatewise,

\[
c_i=b(q_i,r_i),
\]

then for any fixed coordinate \(i\), \(c_i\) depends solely on \((q_i,r_i)\).

Define an admissible reference set \(R_i\subseteq\mathcal Q\), and the coordinate-level inverse possibility set:

\[
\operatorname{Poss}(c_i,R_i)
=
\{q\in\mathcal Q:
\exists r\in R_i,\ b(q,r)=c_i\}.
\]

This definition contains no global support size \(K\). Therefore:

> **Proposition.** Under purely coordinate-separable binding and coordinate-separable partial unbinding, with no normalization, global top-\(K\) projection, superposition, collision resolution, cleanup-memory query, thresholding, cross-coordinate coding constraint, or other global downstream operation, partial-unbind ambiguity at coordinate \(i\) is independent of the number of active coordinates elsewhere in the vector.

### Proof

Let two vector pairs \((q,r)\) and \((q',r')\) have the same values at coordinate \(i\):

\[
q_i=q'_i,\qquad r_i=r'_i.
\]

They may have arbitrary and different active counts outside coordinate \(i\). By coordinate separability,

\[
c_i=b(q_i,r_i)=b(q'_i,r'_i)=c'_i.
\]

Because the partial-unbind possibility set is computed from \(c_i\), \(R_i\), and the scalar function \(b\),

\[
\operatorname{Poss}(c_i,R_i)
=
\operatorname{Poss}(c'_i,R_i).
\]

Thus its cardinality is identical irrespective of support values at all coordinates \(j\neq i\). QED.

This is a strong negative result for H3 as phrased: **global occupancy cannot itself make a coordinate-level partial inverse less recoverable in a strictly coordinate-separable pipeline.**

### Where cross-coordinate dependence could enter

The conclusion changes only if Nexus introduces an actual operation coupling coordinates. Potential mechanisms include:

| Mechanism | How \(K\) could matter |
|---|---|
| Bundling/summation | Other vectors contribute cross-talk to a coordinate or score |
| Global top-\(K\) | A coordinate can be discarded because another coordinate ranks higher |
| Normalization | Coordinate values or thresholds depend on whole-vector norm |
| Cleanup memory / ANN | Candidate selection depends on whole-vector similarity |
| Quantization/clipping after a global aggregate | Error behavior depends on collective amplitude distribution |
| Block-code constraint | Coordinates are jointly constrained by block occupancy |
| Error-correcting or constrained code | Validity of a coordinate can depend on other coordinates |
| Resonator/recurrent reconstruction | Global iterative state influences local estimates |

These are not inferences about Nexus. They are the required implementation-audit checklist. An external study of sparse VSA binding reinforces the distinction: sparsity-preserving vector binding may require a reduction operation and can be lossy unless special structural assumptions hold. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12180425/)

### Coordinate-level ambiguity examples

Suppose all reference values are allowed:

\[
R=\{-2,-1,0,+1,+2\}.
\]

Then:

- If \(c=0\), every \(q\in\mathcal Q\) is possible because choosing \(r=0\) yields zero. Thus \(|\operatorname{Poss}(0,R)|=5\).
- If \(c=+2\), only \(q=+2\) can produce it, because \(\min(|q|,|r|)=2\) requires \(|q|=|r|=2\) and positive product. Thus \(\operatorname{Poss}(+2,R)=\{+2\}\).
- If \(c=+1\), both \(q=+1\) and \(q=+2\) can be possible with suitable positive \(r\), so the inverse is ambiguous.

Under a restricted known reference value \(r\), the possibilities change. For example:

- \(r=+2\), \(c=+2\) implies \(q=+2\) exactly.
- \(r=+2\), \(c=+1\) implies \(q=+1\) exactly.
- \(r=+1\), \(c=+1\) permits \(q=+1\) or \(q=+2\), because the minimum magnitude is one in both cases.

Thus the ambiguity is driven by the finite scalar algebra and what is known about \(r_i\), not by remote coordinates’ occupancy.

## Exact, ambiguous, unknown, impossible

### Possibility-set semantics

For a finite possibility construction:

\[
\operatorname{Poss}(c,R)
=
\{q\in\mathcal Q:\exists r\in R,\ b(q,r)=c\},
\]

the natural classification is:

| Cardinality | State |
|---:|---|
| \(\left|\operatorname{Poss}\right|=0\) | IMPOSSIBLE |
| \(\left|\operatorname{Poss}\right|=1\) | EXACT |
| \(\left|\operatorname{Poss}\right|>1\) | AMBIGUOUS |

`UNKNOWN` does **not** automatically arise from this construction. In a finite exhaustive algebra with known \(c\), known \(R\), and a fully specified binding law, the possibility set is always computable and falls into one of the three cases above.

Therefore, one should not say that EXACT / AMBIGUOUS / UNKNOWN / IMPOSSIBLE are all generated by one finite possibility-set construction unless Nexus defines an additional semantic layer.

### A rigorous four-state extension

There are defensible ways to model four epistemic conditions, but they must be explicitly specified.

One option is to separate:

1. **object-level feasibility** — the set of values consistent with the algebra; and
2. **meta-level knowledge** — whether the system has enough information to compute or trust that set.

For example:

| State | Formal meaning |
|---|---|
| EXACT | A known singleton possibility set |
| AMBIGUOUS | A known non-singleton, nonempty possibility set |
| IMPOSSIBLE | A known empty possibility set |
| UNKNOWN | The possibility set is unavailable, not computable, input data are missing, reference domain is unspecified, or a prior stage reported an unknown state |

A powerset domain \(\mathcal P(\mathcal Q)\), ordered by information content, can model empty, singleton, and multi-value results. An extra “not evaluated/unavailable” element can represent UNKNOWN. Alternatively, a bilattice or Belnap-style framework can represent lack of information and inconsistency separately; Belnap’s well-known four-valued construction distinguishes “neither” from “both” and has a separate information/knowledge order. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20679v1)

But do not equate `UNKNOWN` with `AMBIGUOUS`:

- **AMBIGUOUS** means the system knows multiple values remain feasible.
- **UNKNOWN** means the system cannot establish the feasible-value set, or has no adequate observation/model.

That distinction is important for HTLA claims.

## Information theory of the stated binding

### Coordinate model

Let \(Q,R\in\{-2,-1,0,+1,+2\}\) be independent. Let

\[
p=\Pr(Q\neq0)=\Pr(R\neq0),
\]

and, conditional on being nonzero, assume signs are equiprobable and magnitudes \(1,2\) are equiprobable. Thus:

\[
\Pr(Q=0)=1-p,\qquad
\Pr(Q=s)=\frac p4
\]

for each nonzero symbol \(s\).

Then

\[
C=b(Q,R)
\]

is nonzero only when both operands are nonzero:

\[
\Pr(C\neq0)=p^2.
\]

The output probabilities are:

\[
\Pr(C=0)=1-p^2,
\]

\[
\Pr(C=+2)=\Pr(C=-2)=\frac{p^2}{16},
\]

\[
\Pr(C=+1)=\Pr(C=-1)=\frac{7p^2}{16}.
\]

The value \(+1\), for example, has seven of sixteen equally likely nonzero-pair preimages: all positive-sign magnitude combinations except \((2,2)\).

### Entropies

The entropy of one input coordinate is

\[
H(Q)=h_2(p)+2p,
\]

where \(h_2\) is binary entropy. At \(p=307/1024\approx0.2998046875\),

\[
H(Q)\approx1.481\ \text{bits per coordinate}.
\]

The conditional information retained about \(Q\) from \(C\) when \(R\) is known is:

\[
I(Q;C\mid R)
=
p\left[h_2(p)+\frac{3p}{2}\right].
\]

At \(p=307/1024\),

\[
I(Q;C\mid R)\approx0.556\ \text{bits per coordinate}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
H(Q\mid C,R)
=
H(Q)-I(Q;C\mid R)
\approx0.925\ \text{bits per coordinate}.
\]

These figures are not properties of Nexus absent confirmation of the assumed distribution. They show a central structural point: the amount of retained information changes **smoothly** with \(p\); there is no singularity, discontinuity, or privileged value at 0.30 in this coordinatewise stochastic model.

### Meaning of the result

A high output occupancy is not guaranteed by high input occupancy because the binding rule vacuums a coordinate when either operand is zero:

\[
p_{\text{output}}=p_Qp_R.
\]

If both inputs have occupancy \(p=0.30\), a random independent bound result has only about:

\[
p^2\approx0.09
\]

nonzero occupancy before any subsequent operation. For \(D=1024\), that is approximately 92 nonzero output coordinates—numerically the same as the expected support overlap because both derive from \(D p^2\).

This fact could matter operationally if Nexus expects the binder itself to preserve a 30% active count. Under the stated rule and independent supports, it does not: it produces roughly 9% active output. Whether that is desired, corrected by a later projection, or irrelevant cannot be determined without implementation access.

### Collision distinctions

There are two different collision questions:

1. **Scalar pair collisions:** many pairs \((q,r)\) map to the same scalar composite \(c\). These are intrinsic to `min`-magnitude binding and exist regardless of global \(K\).
2. **Whole-vector collisions:** two distinct vector pairs \((q,r)\neq(q',r')\) produce the same full composite vector. Under independent coordinate sampling, whole-vector collision probabilities compound over coordinates and can vary with occupancy because occupancy changes the coordinate distribution.

Neither fact creates an abrupt threshold at \(K=307\). A whole-vector collision criterion could justify a cap only after specifying:

- the input distribution;
- whether supports are independent;
- the acceptable collision probability;
- the number of vectors/pairs in the workload;
- whether collisions mean exact equality, cleanup confusion, or semantic aliasing;
- all post-binding operations.

## External HDC and SDM comparison

### HDC/VSA literature

The public literature supports a disciplined separation between binding, superposition, and cleanup:

- Classical VSA operations use binding to create a composite dissimilar to its inputs, and addition/bundling to create a composite similar to its constituents. The difficulty of reliable retrieval from a superposition increases with the number of combined items relative to vector dimension. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9869149/)
- The standard HDC framing does not give a universal activation fraction. It treats vector width, code distribution, similarity metric, codebook size, noise, number of bundled items, and task demands as design parameters. [dl.acm](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3538531)
- Sparse VSA binding is model-specific. General sparse operations can be lossy; special block-sparse codes can have ideal lossless unbinding properties, but at the cost of an imposed block structure and reduced coding entropy compared with unrestricted \(K\)-sparse vectors. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12180425/)

The available literature does **not** establish:

\[
K/D\approx0.30
\]

as a general optimum for quinary Hadamard-like binding, sparse VSA, HDC capacity, bundling, cleanup memory, or symbolic recovery.

### Sparse Distributed Memory

Kanerva-style SDM uses “sparse” in ways that can differ from Nexus coordinate sparsity:

| Form of sparsity | Meaning | Equivalent to Nexus 30% coordinate occupancy? |
|---|---|---:|
| Address activation sparsity | Fraction of memory hard locations activated by an address | No |
| Sparse coordinate representation | Few nonzero components in a vector | Potentially, but only if Nexus uses this exact model |
| Sparse memory locations | Tiny instantiated subset of a vast address space | No |
| Sparse connectivity | Limited synapses/fan-in/fan-out | No |
| Sparse retrieval/cell assembly | Small activated subset during recall | No |

Classical SDM examples choose access radii to activate a small fraction of hard locations. One cited analysis describes an \(n=1000\) memory with a radius selected to activate roughly \(1/1000\) of a sample of hard locations—this is a retrieval-address mechanism, not a rule that 30% of vector coordinates should be nonzero. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4009432/)

The same literature’s critical-distance behavior depends on a specific combination of dimension, number of hard locations, access radius, number of stored patterns, write strength, and iterative read dynamics.  It is therefore not evidence for a universal Nexus `K_wall = 307`. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4009432/)

### k-WTA comparison

k-WTA enforces cardinality by retaining the \(K\) largest activations. It can be an architectural policy, a regularizer, a coding mechanism, or a computational device, depending on implementation.

However:

- \(k\)-WTA output sparsity is not automatically sparse computation.
- If all \(D=1024\) scores must be computed and sorted/selected before retaining the top 307, the representation is sparse but much of the computation is still dense.
- A 30% retained fraction is relatively dense compared with biological-style sparse coding discussions, where activation fractions are often described as only a few percent; it may still be “sparse” relative to a fully active quinary vector. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12180425/)
- The generic k-WTA literature does not supply a theorem that 30% is the correct retention ratio for a 1,024-coordinate quinary HSL system. [proceedings.neurips](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/1988/hash/6c4b761a28b734fe93831e3fb400ce87-Abstract.html)

Thus, if Nexus uses top-307 selection, the evidence would initially support “a chosen k-WTA policy,” not “a mathematical wall.”

## Packing and ANN audit

### Packing / bit budget

The supplied representation suggests three conceptual bit planes:

- sign;
- magnitude-1;
- magnitude-2.

But this does not by itself establish a 307-related storage threshold.

If the system uses fixed dense bit planes, then each vector’s packed size is controlled by \(D\), not active count \(K\). For \(D=1024\), three one-bit planes would suggest a fixed 3,072-bit / 384-byte raw plane budget, before headers, alignment, validity masks, or compression. Under that scheme:

\[
\text{packed bytes are independent of }K.
\]

A \(K\)-dependent storage justification becomes plausible only if code uses a sparse encoding such as:

- a list of active indices plus quinary values;
- run-length encoding;
- bitmap plus value payload;
- variable-width blocks;
- an inline representation below a threshold and dense representation above it;
- a fixed 307-entry active-index structure;
- page, cache-line, SIMD, or serialization boundaries tied to 307.

No such mechanism was available to inspect. Therefore:

> “A fixed per-coordinate bit budget causes recoverability degradation beyond 307” is unsupported by the presently available evidence.

At most, 307 could be a policy selected to control sparse-storage size or runtime—if implementation proves that a \(K\)-dependent sparse encoding actually exists.

### ANN / HNSW implications

For independently generated \(K\)-sparse vectors, occupancy affects similarity distributions continuously because expected support overlap scales as \(K^2/D\). It can therefore affect:

- cosine-score variance;
- support-overlap score distributions;
- candidate contrast;
- index quantization behavior;
- sparse dot-product runtime;
- HNSW graph construction/query behavior.

But a specific claim that \(K\approx307\) improves HNSW recall or nearest-neighbor separability requires a benchmark with:

- data distribution;
- query distribution;
- distance/similarity metric;
- index parameters such as `M`, `efConstruction`, `efSearch`;
- codebook size;
- vector encoding;
- exact-search baseline;
- Recall@\(k\), latency, memory, and hubness measurements.

No general HDC/VSA theorem converts “30% sparse” into “better ANN.” In particular, ANN retrieval quality and symbolic partial-unbinding recoverability are different empirical questions.

## Required empirical program

A defensible Nexus conclusion should use controlled experiments rather than point measurements at 307.

### Sweep design

For each:

\[
K\in\{16,32,64,96,128,160,192,224,256,288,300,306,307,308,320,352,384,448,512,640,768,896,1024\},
\]

hold all non-\(K\) parameters fixed and repeat over multiple random seeds and representative data.

Measure:

| Domain | Minimum measurements |
|---|---|
| Representation validity | packed round-trip; sign/vacuum violations; magnitude-plane violations |
| Binding | output occupancy; per-coordinate collision distribution; whole-vector duplicate/collision rate; entropy |
| Partial unbinding | exact, ambiguous, impossible, and separately defined unknown fractions |
| Whole-vector recovery | coordinate accuracy; exact-vector recovery; cleanup success if applicable |
| Similarity | random-pair distribution; class contrast if labels exist; hubness |
| ANN | exact-vs-approximate Recall@\(k\); index size; build time; query latency |
| Runtime | bind; unbind; k-WTA; similarity; serialization |
| Storage | dense packed bytes; sparse encoded bytes; index bytes |

### Threshold test

To justify “wall” terminology, test whether performance exhibits a localized change around 307 rather than a smooth trend.

At minimum:

1. Fit a monotone smooth model across \(p=K/D\).
2. Fit segmented models with candidate breakpoints near 307.
3. Bootstrap confidence intervals for the breakpoint.
4. Compare models using held-out error or an appropriate information criterion.
5. Test local contrasts: 300 vs. 307, 306 vs. 307, 307 vs. 308, and 307 vs. 320.
6. Predefine an engineering acceptance criterion, such as exact recovery \(\ge99.9\%\), Recall@10 \(\ge99\%\), or maximum serialized size.

A smooth decline does not validate a hard threshold. It supports language such as “reference operating cap selected to satisfy criterion X.”

## Recommendation for HTLA

### Terminology

Until code history and evidence establish a genuine discontinuity, avoid `K_wall`. “Wall” implies a hard mathematical, representation-level, or verified safety boundary. None has been demonstrated here.

Prefer one of:

- `K_ref = 307`;
- `reference active-coordinate cap`;
- `reference occupancy cap`;
- `maximum active-coordinate policy`;
- `reference sparsity constraint`.

Reserve “wall” for a value supported by a theorem, an encoding limit, or a pre-registered empirical acceptance boundary.

### What the paper may say now

The canonical paper should distinguish the invariant from its rationale:

- The invariant is: reference HSL vectors must have no more than 307 active coordinates.
- The numerical fact is: this equals 29.98046875% maximum occupancy and at least 70.01953125% zero coordinates.
- The unproven claims are: improved recoverability, a capacity theorem, a universal sparse-HDC optimum, and a sharp degradation point.

### Exact insertion sentence

> **“The current 1,024-dimensional quinary HSL reference profile enforces an implementation-level cap of \(K_{\mathrm{ref}}=307\) active coordinates—equivalent to at most 29.98046875% occupancy and at least 70.01953125% zero coordinates; this value is a reference design constraint rather than a claimed universal recoverability threshold or mathematical optimum.”**











**Why 307?**
It is a **versioned reference occupancy cap / soft design target** of the current Nexus v4 quinary HSL geometry (`D=1024`, alphabet `Q={-2,-1,0,+1,+2}`), chosen so that active coordinates stay at ~30% and structural vacuum at ~70%. It is **not** a mathematical theorem, a universal HDC/VSA optimum, a sharp recoverability phase transition, or a bit-budget necessity derived from first principles. Evidence classifies it as an **implementation constant / empirical operating point / policy threshold** (primarily H1 + H9 + partial H10), later rationalized in drafts as the point where coordinate-level partial-unbind classification remains reliable under the fixed three-plane encoding.

### 1. Executive conclusion
- **Hard-coded constant**: `K_WALL = 307` appears directly in the canonical reference implementation (`hsl.py`).
- **Arithmetic origin**: Exactly `floor(0.3 × 1024) = 307` (or nearest integer to 30% occupancy). Vacuum fraction = `1 - 307/1024 = 0.7001953125`.
- **Provenance**: First explicit appearances in mid-to-late August 2026 corpus documents (identity-architecture master paper, architecture spreadsheets, HTLA Tier-2 drafts). Later documents largely copy the value; no pre-August independent derivation or benchmark suite establishing optimality was recovered.
- **Role**: Enforced by k-WTA projection after binding/bundling/continuous-to-quinary conversion. It is a policy limit that keeps vectors inside the tested operating regime of the bit-plane representation and the four-state partial-unbind classifier.
- **What it is not**: A hard mathematical limit, a statistically identified change-point unique to 307, a capacity maximizer from classical Kanerva SDM / sparse binary VSA theory, or a packing/SIMD boundary that forces exactly 307 actives.

The HTLA paper should therefore treat it as an **application-specific reference constraint**, not a deep invariant.

## 3. Mathematical Analysis of K-Sparse Quinary Capacity

For exactly K active coordinates in D dimensions with alphabet {-2, -1, +1, +2}:

**N(D,K) = C(D,K) × 4^K**

|---|-----|-------------|------------------------|

| 512 | 50% | 2042.7 | ~0% |
| 768 | 75% | 2361.6 | ~0% |
| 1024 | 100% | 2048.0 | 100% (cumulative) |

## Executive conclusion

### Why 307?

**The best-supported answer is that 307 is a historical/reference k-WTA occupancy cap obtained by taking the earlier 30%-active/70%-vacuum design target and discretizing it as \(\lfloor0.30\times1024\rfloor=307\). I found no evidence that 307 was independently discovered as a recoverability threshold, collision threshold, capacity optimum, ANN optimum, packing boundary, or mathematical constant.**

The provenance is unusually revealing:

* A **70% sparsity target predates 307 by many months** in the accessible Nexus corpus. A September 9, 2025 TMS engineering document says the “70% sparsity constraint from the CA” can be enforced for regularization and describes the intended benefits as robustness and energy efficiency; it does not derive 70% mathematically. fileciteturn15file3L28-L39 fileciteturn19file0
* By September 22, 2025, a Nexus/HTNLA plan explicitly equates `k_factor = 0.3` with **70% sparsity** and proposes k-WTA as the enforcement mechanism. fileciteturn16file0 fileciteturn16file1
* \(1024\) itself appears in the corpus first as one possible size in a **scalable range of 1024–16384 dimensions**, rather than as a demonstrated optimum; by June 5, 2026, a document treats the state space as \(\mathbb R^{1024}\). fileciteturn29file0L1-L9 fileciteturn28file0L1-L11
* A June 25 revision of the design debate **before 307 appears** explicitly criticizes the “70% thermodynamic wall” story, saying that 70% is not shown to be a derived constant and describing that explanation as post-hoc. fileciteturn23file0
* A later revision the same day introduces the explicit controller formula \(k_{\rm allow}=\min(307,\ldots)\) and describes 307 as the **30% active cap**. fileciteturn24file0
* By July 18, downstream documents make the arithmetic explicit: one says \(307/1024\approx30\%\), matching the previously stated target, while another gives \(k=\lfloor0.3\times1024\rfloor\approx307\). fileciteturn12file0L1-L5 fileciteturn12file4L30-L38
* The July 21 reference code then freezes `DIM = 1024` and `K_WALL = 307`, with the comment “<=30% retained activation, >=70% structural vacuum.” fileciteturn12file2L12-L20

That order strongly favors **“70%/30% target first → 307 derived afterward”**, not “307 experimentally discovered → 70% inferred afterward.”

The implementation reinforces that interpretation. In the current reference code, 307 is hard-coded and used by the controller to clamp its calculated `k_allow`; the representation itself can contain more than 307 active coordinates. The constructor does not reject them, packing size is unaffected by \(K\), and `apply_k_wta` itself accepts a requested \(k\) anywhere from 0 to 1024. The normal runtime reaches the cap through controller policy. fileciteturn8file2

Most importantly, **partial unbinding supplies no mathematical reason for 307**. The reference binding rule is coordinate-separable. For fixed \(c_i,r_i\), the possible values of \(q_i\) do not depend on how many other coordinates are active. Global occupancy changes how often various coordinate cases occur, but it does not change the local inverse relation, and nothing discontinuous occurs at \(K=307\).

I also executed the downloaded reference implementation's own property suite; it completed with `All Project Nexus v4.0 property tests passed.` The suite tests deterministic k-WTA at \(k=123\), not a failure boundary at 307, and source search shows `K_WALL` only in the constant definition, calibration fingerprint, and controller clipping expression. Thus the current tests demonstrate implementation behavior, **not why the threshold should be 307**. The executable artifact is the same July 21 reference file cited above. fileciteturn25file0L3-L19

The external literature points in the same direction. Sparse HDC/VSA representations can indeed have important memory, noise, or implementation advantages, but their useful sparsity depends on the representation, binding operation, cleanup memory, load, noise and decoding procedure. Frady, Kleyko and Sommer explicitly formulate sparsity as \(K/N\), analyze model-dependent sparse binding, and give recovery conditions involving \(K\), dimension and the measurement/codebook structure rather than a universal 30% ratio. citeturn6view0 Winner-take-all methods deliberately impose sparsity, but the original WTA-autoencoder work treats sparsity as a chosen learning mechanism rather than establishing a universal 30% law. citeturn7view0 Classical VSA superposition capacity is likewise governed by crosstalk, dimensionality and load. citeturn7view3

So the evidentiary classification is:

> **307 is presently a useful, deterministic reference-policy constant with historical provenance, but not a demonstrated mathematical wall or empirically established optimum.**

## Internal provenance and implementation audit

### The provenance timeline

The important finding is not merely that earlier files contain 70% and later files contain 307; it is that the documents progressively expose the transformation from a qualitative target into an integer implementation constant.

```mermaid
timeline
    title Nexus 70% → 30% → 307 provenance
    2025-09-09 : 70% sparsity appears as a CA-derived design constraint
               : rationale stated as robustness / energy efficiency
    2025-09-22 : k-WTA proposed as enforcement mechanism
               : k_factor = 0.3 explicitly means 70% sparsity
    2026-02 : 1024 appears within a scalable 1024–16384-D range
    2026-06-05 : R^1024 treated as a concrete Nexus state space
               : 70% sparsity still used as a recovery/control action
    2026-06-25 early : design critique says 70% is not shown as a derived constant
    2026-06-25 later : 307 appears as explicit 30%-active controller cap
    2026-07-18 : 307/1024 explicitly connected to the prior 30/70 target
               : floor(0.3 × 1024) explicitly stated
    2026-07-21 : reference implementation hard-codes DIM=1024, K_WALL=307
```

The September 2025 source describes 70% as a CA constraint and discusses it in regularization/energy terms, but does not show an optimization, theorem, benchmark curve or measured breakpoint. fileciteturn19file0 The later September plan is even more explicit operationally: it gives a k-WTA function whose `k_factor` example is 0.3 “for 70% sparsity.” fileciteturn16file1

That significantly weakens the hypothesis that 307 emerged from an experiment. In the June 25 revision history, revision 9 contains no “307” and explicitly challenges 70% as a mathematically derived wall; by revision 61 the explicit \(307\) controller cap is present. fileciteturn23file0 fileciteturn24file0 The subsequent July documents then state the arithmetic relationship rather than an independent optimization result. fileciteturn12file0L1-L5 fileciteturn12file4L30-L38

This is not proof that no still-earlier rationale exists outside the connected corpus. It is, however, strong evidence for the provenance visible today: **the percentage target is historically upstream of the exact integer**.

### Floor or round?

Exactly,

\[
0.30(1024)=307.2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\lfloor0.30(1024)\rfloor=307,
\]

while ordinary nearest-integer rounding also happens to produce 307. The decisive provenance evidence is the July 18 source that explicitly writes the operation as **floor**, not merely round. fileciteturn12file4L30-L38

The resulting exact reference fractions are

\[
p_{\rm ref}={307\over1024}=0.2998046875,
\]

and

\[
v_{\rm ref}=1-p_{\rm ref}
={717\over1024}
=0.7001953125.
\]

Thus the slight excess over 70% vacuum is a consequence of integer quantization, not evidence for a distinct 70.0195% optimum.

### What the current code actually does

The current implementation uses three packed bit planes—sign, magnitude-1 and magnitude-2—with the invariant that the two magnitude planes cannot overlap. `K_WALL` does **not** define the alphabet, bit-plane layout or validity of the quinary representation. fileciteturn8file0

Its operative dependency is:

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    K["K_WALL = 307"]
    F["Calibration fingerprint"]
    C["Controller"]
    A["k_allow = clip(floor(...), 1, K_WALL)"]
    W["apply_k_wta(k_allow, salience)"]
    G["Gated composite"]
    U["partial_unbind(binder)"]
    M["semantic_snap / cleanup"]
    R["guard / runtime result"]

    K --> F
    K --> C
    C --> A
    A --> W
    W --> G
    G --> U
    U --> M
    M --> R
```

The controller computes an affine resource budget from its control state and then clips the result to `K_WALL`; this makes 307 a **controller policy ceiling**. fileciteturn8file2 The runtime then applies deterministic salience-ranked k-WTA before partial unbinding and semantic cleanup. fileciteturn8file1

This creates an important distinction:

**Direct effect:**
\(K_{\rm wall}\rightarrow k_{\rm allow}\rightarrow\) how many active coordinates survive global competition.

**Indirect downstream effect:**
If k-WTA deletes an active coordinate before partial unbinding, the later inverse sees a different \(c_i\), so recovery can worsen.

**No direct algebraic effect:**
`partial_unbind(c,r)` itself does not inspect `K_WALL`, total popcount, or occupancy elsewhere in the vector. fileciteturn8file0

That distinction resolves an apparent contradiction. It is entirely possible for global top-\(k\) pruning to affect whole-vector reconstruction while the **coordinatewise inverse relation itself remains occupancy-independent**.

### Packing and the “bit-budget” hypothesis

With \(D=1024\), `WORD_BITS=64` gives 16 machine words per plane. Three 1024-bit planes therefore contain

\[
3\times1024=3072\ {\rm bits}
=384\ {\rm raw\ bytes}.
\]

That is fixed whether a vector has 1, 307, 700 or 1024 active coordinates. The inspected representation contains no separate coordinate-list encoding whose size switches at 307. `K_WALL` is present in calibration/controller logic, not in the packing calculation. fileciteturn12file2L12-L20 fileciteturn8file2

Accordingly, the claim that 307 follows from a per-coordinate bit budget, page boundary, SIMD boundary, or packed-vector size is **unsupported by the current reference implementation**. A sparse-result policy can reduce downstream activity, but in this implementation it does not reduce the number of bits in the underlying packed HSL object.

## Mathematical capacity, overlap, collision, and recoverability

### Raw combinatorial capacity

For exactly \(K\) active coordinates and four possible nonzero symbols,

\[
N(D,K)=\binom DK4^K,
\]

so

\[
\log_2N(D,K)
=\log_2\binom DK+2K.
\]

This is only a **representation state-count calculation**; it is not a retrieval-capacity theorem.

For \(D=1024\):

| \(K\) | Occupancy | \(\log_2 N(1024,K)\) bits | Expected support overlap \(K^2/D\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 32 | 3.13% | 265.6 | 1.0 |
| 64 | 6.25% | 469.1 | 4.0 |
| 128 | 12.50% | 807.9 | 16.0 |
| 192 | 18.75% | 1,092.0 | 36.0 |
| 256 | 25.00% | 1,337.6 | 64.0 |
| **307** | **29.98%** | **1,511.0** | **92.04** |
| 384 | 37.50% | 1,740.1 | 144.0 |
| 512 | 50.00% | 2,042.7 | 256.0 |
| 768 | 75.00% | 2,361.6 | 576.0 |
| 819 | 79.98% | 2,372.65 | 655.04 |
| 820 | 80.08% | 2,372.65 | 656.64 |
| 1024 | 100% | 2,048.0 | 1,024 |

The adjacent-shell ratio is

\[
{N(D,K+1)\over N(D,K)}
=
4{D-K\over K+1}.
\]

For \(D=1024\), it equals one at \(K=819\), making \(K=819\) and \(820\) the equal maximizing shells. **Nothing extremal happens at 307.**

The cumulative state space allowed by \(K\le307\) has

\[
\log_2\sum_{k=0}^{307}\binom{1024}{k}4^k
\approx1511.16\ {\rm bits}.
\]

The unrestricted quinary space has

\[
\log_2 5^{1024}
=1024\log_2 5
\approx2377.65\ {\rm bits}.
\]

Thus the \(\le307\) constraint retains about **63.6% of the unrestricted logarithmic raw state capacity**. Again, this says nothing by itself about semantic retrieval quality or cleanup capacity.

A related i.i.d. model with active probability \(p\) and all four nonzero values equiprobable has per-coordinate entropy

\[
H(X)=h_2(p)+2p.
\]

This is maximized at \(p=0.8\), where all five quinary symbols become equiprobable. At the Nexus reference \(p=307/1024\),

\[
H(X)\approx1.481\ {\rm bits/coordinate},
\]

not an extremum. Raw information theory therefore supplies no 30% preference.

### Support overlap

Two independent uniformly selected \(K\)-subsets have

\[
X=|A\cap B|
\sim\operatorname{Hypergeometric}(N=D,\;K,\;n=K)
\]

with

\[
E[X]={K^2\over D}
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{Var}(X)
=
K{K\over D}\left(1-{K\over D}\right)
{D-K\over D-1}.
\]

At \(D=1024,K=307\),

\[
E[X]\approx92.04,\qquad
\sigma_X\approx6.72.
\]

The probability that two supports have **zero** overlap is

\[
P(X=0)=
{\binom{D-K}{K}\over\binom DK},
\]

which at \(K=307\) is approximately

\[
1.17\times10^{-59}.
\]

So 30% occupancy is not “sparse” in the sense of making independently generated supports usually disjoint. It creates roughly 92 shared active positions per random pair. At \(K=32\), by contrast, the expected overlap is 1 and the probability of no overlap is about 35.6%.

That is not inherently bad. HDC/VSA similarity usually relies on cancellation/concentration rather than literal support disjointness. The sparse-VSA literature explicitly distinguishes sparse representation structure from the behavior of the binding and cleanup mechanisms. citeturn6view0

### Coordinatewise binding proves the key independence result

The current conservative quinary binding is

\[
f(q,r)=
\begin{cases}
0,&q=0\ \text{or}\ r=0,\\
\operatorname{sign}(qr)\min(|q|,|r|),&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Define

\[
\operatorname{Poss}(c,r)
=
\{q\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}:f(q,r)=c\}.
\]

For an exact known binder:

| Binder state | Composite state | Possibility set | Epistemic result |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(r=0\) | \(c=0\) | all five \(q\) values | UNKNOWN |
| \(r=0\) | \(c\ne0\) | \(\varnothing\) | IMPOSSIBLE |
| \(|r|=1\) | \(c=0\) | \(\{0\}\) | EXACT |
| \(|r|=1\) | sign-compatible \(|c|=1\) | two magnitudes, \(|q|=1\) or 2 | AMBIGUOUS |
| \(|r|=1\) | \(|c|=2\) | \(\varnothing\) | IMPOSSIBLE |
| \(|r|=2\) | \(c=0\) | \(\{0\}\) | EXACT |
| \(|r|=2\) | nonzero valid \(c\) | one corresponding \(q\) | EXACT |

This yields a direct proof.

Let the vector binding be coordinate-separable:

\[
F(q,r)_i=f(q_i,r_i).
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{Poss}_i(c_i,r_i)
=
f_{r_i}^{-1}(c_i)
\]

contains no term involving \(q_j,r_j,c_j\) for \(j\ne i\), nor \(K=\|q\|_0\). Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{For coordinate-separable binding, local partial-unbind ambiguity at }i
\text{ is independent of global occupancy }K
}
\]

**unless an additional global constraint or operation is imposed.**

The reference code's `partial_unbind` matches precisely this local logic: unknown positions are determined by inactive binder coordinates, ambiguity by active magnitude-1 binder coordinates, exact magnitudes by magnitude-2 binder coordinates, and impossible patterns by locally inconsistent composite/binder combinations. fileciteturn8file0

Global Nexus mechanisms *can* introduce cross-coordinate dependence before or after this operation—particularly k-WTA and semantic cleanup—but that is a different statement from claiming the inverse mapping itself fails past 307.

### The four epistemic states

The current four labels can be embedded rigorously in a powerset domain over

\[
Q=\{-2,-1,0,+1,+2\}.
\]

Under the information ordering “smaller candidate set = more information”:

\[
\varnothing \rightarrow \text{IMPOSSIBLE},
\]

\[
|\operatorname{Poss}|=1
\rightarrow \text{EXACT},
\]

\[
1<|\operatorname{Poss}|<|Q|
\rightarrow \text{AMBIGUOUS},
\]

\[
\operatorname{Poss}=Q
\rightarrow \text{UNKNOWN}.
\]

That is cleaner than forcing the four words themselves into an ad-hoc four-element logic. The full powerset \(\mathcal P(Q)\) is a lattice; **EXACT and AMBIGUOUS are classes of elements within it**, not individual lattice points. Consequently, the four summary labels are not themselves closed under arbitrary meet/join operations unless additional abstraction rules are specified.

This also explains why `UNKNOWN` is structurally different in the implementation: when the binder is vacuum, the composite reveals nothing about \(q_i\); the candidate set is all of \(Q\). fileciteturn8file0

### Binding-output and collision model

Let each coordinate be active independently with probability \(p\), signs be equiprobable, and let a nonzero input have magnitude 2 with probability \(h\). The current reference generator uses `p_zero=0.70` and `p_mag2_given_active=0.15`, so its default model is approximately \(p=0.30,h=0.15\). fileciteturn8file0

Because a bound coordinate is active only if **both** operands are active,

\[
P(C\ne0)=p^2.
\]

Conditional on activity, output magnitude 2 occurs only if both inputs have magnitude 2, hence with probability \(h^2\). Therefore

\[
P(C=0)=1-p^2,
\]

\[
P(C=\pm1)
={p^2(1-h^2)\over2},
\]

\[
P(C=\pm2)
={p^2h^2\over2}.
\]

This gives

\[
H(C)
=
h_2(p^2)
+p^2\left[1+h_2(h^2)\right].
\]

Every term is smooth in \(p\). Nothing singular occurs at \(p=0.30\).

For two independently generated composites, the per-coordinate equality probability is

\[
s(p,h)
=
(1-p^2)^2
+
{p^4\over2}
\left[(1-h^2)^2+h^4\right].
\]

Under independent coordinates, whole-vector equality probability is \(s^{1024}\). For the reference \(h=0.15\), its collision entropy at \(p=307/1024\) is approximately 0.265 bits per coordinate, or about 271 bits over 1024 coordinates. The collision probability is already extraordinarily small under this simple i.i.d. whole-vector model, and it changes continuously rather than crossing a special boundary at 30%.

At very low occupancies, an important opposite phenomenon appears: many bindings collapse to an all-zero or nearly-zero composite because active supports rarely intersect. For exact \(K=32\) supports, for example, a pair has no active overlap about 35.6% of the time. Thus “sparser is always better for collision resistance” is false.

### Conditional information retained by binding

Under the same \(p,h\) model,

\[
H(Q)
=
h_2(p)+p[1+h_2(h)].
\]

Given the binder \(R\):

* \(R=0\) erases all information about \(Q\);
* an active magnitude-1 binder preserves zero/nonzero state and sign but loses the constituent's magnitude distinction;
* an active magnitude-2 binder preserves the constituent exactly.

Hence

\[
H(Q\mid C,R)
=
(1-p)H(Q)
+
p^2(1-h)h_2(h),
\]

and

\[
I(Q;C\mid R)
=
H(Q)-H(Q\mid C,R).
\]

With the reference \(h=0.15\) and \(p=307/1024\),

\[
H(Q)\approx1.364\text{ bits},
\]

\[
H(Q\mid C,R)\approx1.001\text{ bits},
\]

\[
I(Q;C\mid R)\approx0.362\text{ bits},
\]

so roughly 26.6% of constituent entropy is retained per coordinate under this particular random-channel model.

Again, the dependence on \(p\) is smooth. There is no qualitative transition at \(p=0.30\).

This is consistent with established VSA theory: loss and retrieval capacity depend on the representation, crosstalk, dimension and cleanup method. Plate's HRR work explicitly treats reconstructed constituents as noisy and relies on cleanup memory; it does not propose a universal support-occupancy threshold. citeturn6view4 Frady, Kleyko and Sommer likewise derive superposition capacity from crosstalk/load relationships rather than a fixed coordinate sparsity fraction. citeturn7view3

## External literature and alternative explanations

### What HDC/VSA theory actually supports

High-dimensional distributed representations gain robustness and separability from dimensionality and the statistical properties of their vector ensembles. Kanerva's HDC framework belongs to this tradition of high-dimensional random-vector computation; the relevant theory is representation-specific rather than a prescription that every hypervector should be 30% nonzero. citeturn8search11

Frady, Kleyko and Sommer's sparse-VSA analysis is particularly relevant because it directly studies sparse variable binding. They define sparse codes through \(K/N\), explain that dimensionality-preserving binding for general sparse vectors is necessarily lossy under the constructions studied, and relate recoverability to compressed-sensing-style conditions such as dimension scaling with \(K\log(M/K)\). They also discuss sparse block codes, where code structure changes the trade-off. citeturn6view0 These results support the **general idea that sparsity can matter**, but not the claim that \(K/N\approx0.30\) is universal or optimal.

Classical superposition results similarly emphasize load. The Frady–Kleyko–Sommer theory states that crosstalk limits retrieval and derives capacities for the analyzed networks, including channel capacity on the order of half a bit per neuron in their setting. citeturn7view3 That provides a legitimate framework for justifying an operating point—but to use it for Nexus, one would need to map the exact quinary binding/bundling/cleanup process onto such a model and then measure or derive its load curve. The Nexus corpus does not currently show that derivation.

### Sparse Distributed Memory does not supply a 30/70 law

Kanerva-style Sparse Distributed Memory is often invoked near HDC, but “sparse” there must not be conflated with Nexus coordinate occupancy. Classical SDM stores and retrieves long patterns by distributing them across selected memory locations and using similarity in high-dimensional address space. NASA's historical descriptions emphasize distributed storage, partial-match retrieval, Hamming neighborhoods and activation of subsets of memory locations. citeturn3search0turn3search2turn3search8

That is a different sparsity axis from “70% of the symbols in each stored vector are zero.” Consequently, SDM cannot be cited as evidence for the Nexus 70% structural-vacuum fraction without an explicit model translation.

### k-WTA supports the mechanism, not the number

Winner-take-all competition is a legitimate way to impose sparse activations. Makhzani and Frey's WTA autoencoders directly enforce lifetime and spatial sparsity by winner selection. citeturn7view0

But this supports **H6 as an implementation mechanism**—“a top-\(k\) rule can enforce a chosen sparsity”—not “30% is theoretically privileged.” Sparsity in WTA systems is a design/training parameter whose useful level depends on architecture and task.

The same caveat applies to computational efficiency. A dense salience calculation followed by discarding 70% of coordinates gives a sparse **result**, but does not automatically make preceding computation sparse. The current Nexus `apply_k_wta` first identifies active coordinates and ranks scores before pruning; its existence therefore cannot by itself prove a 70% compute reduction. fileciteturn8file1

### ANN/HNSW does not rescue 307

The original HNSW algorithm is a hierarchical proximity-graph method for approximate nearest-neighbor search in metric spaces; its paper establishes graph construction/search behavior, not a universal optimal sparsity fraction for input vectors. citeturn6view3

For independently generated zero-mean quinary vectors, a useful first-order result is that random cosine concentration is approximately dimension-controlled. If nonzero amplitudes have finite second moment,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\cos\theta)
\approx {1\over D},
\]

because the \(p\)-dependent second moments appear in both dot-product variance and norm normalization and approximately cancel. At \(D=1024\),

\[
\sigma_{\cos}\approx{1\over\sqrt{1024}}=0.03125.
\]

The executable Nexus sweep below reproduces this: random-pair cosine standard deviations remain roughly 0.03 from very sparse through dense cases, including 306, 307 and 308. This does **not** prove that a labeled semantic ANN dataset would show no occupancy effect; it means there is no random-geometry evidence for a special 30% separation advantage.

The inspected Nexus reference file also contains no HNSW implementation, so an exact-vs-HNSW Recall@k benchmark cannot responsibly be attributed to that artifact. A future benchmark needs an actual indexed corpus with semantic labels or controlled planted neighbors.

### The strongest adversarial case for keeping 307

The best argument *against* the “legacy convention” conclusion is not a hidden theorem; it is a systems-engineering argument.

A fixed 30% cap can still be defensible if it simultaneously gives acceptable headroom in several smooth trade-offs: less downstream activity, bounded controller demand, deterministic resource usage, increased vacuum for gating semantics, and protection against very dense states. Internal documents repeatedly associate the sparsity target with energy, regularization, robustness or homeostatic control. fileciteturn19file0 fileciteturn28file0L1-L11 The current controller also makes the cap part of its calibrated deterministic state, so changing it is not semantically free. fileciteturn8file2

That makes **H10—multiple smooth constraints—plausible**.

What is missing is the calibration record that turns “plausible” into “validated”: benchmark curves for 20%, 25%, 30%, 35%, 40%, explicit objective weights, and an acceptance criterion showing why 30% dominates nearby alternatives. Without those, systems considerations explain why a cap might exist, not why the cap should be exactly 307.

### Why a statistical change-point at 307 is rejected

For the random model used above, several curves are analytically smooth:

\[
E[K_C]={K^2\over D},
\]

\[
P(\text{UNKNOWN})=1-p,
\]

and, with \(h=0.15\),

\[
P(\text{AMBIGUOUS})
=(1-h)p^2
=0.85p^2,
\]

\[
P(\text{EXACT})
=p(1-p)+hp^2
=p-0.85p^2.
\]

No segmented-regression model is needed to discover a breakpoint when the exact generating equations contain no breakpoint. The neighborhood results at 300, 306, 307, 308 and 320 agree with those smooth forms.

Thus the statement **“past 307, partial-unbind recoverability fails” is falsified for the coordinate-separable reference binding rule.**

The stronger, more limited statement is defensible:

> “When the controller chooses \(k_{\rm allow}\le307\), global k-WTA deliberately removes excess activity; if valid composite coordinates are removed, subsequent reconstruction can lose information.”

That is a policy consequence, not a mathematical capacity wall.

### What was not benchmarkable

The current artifact contains no actual HNSW index implementation or labeled semantic benchmark, so I did not manufacture Recall@k, hubness or latency claims. The random-cosine experiment answers only the geometry of independent random vectors, not semantic nearest-neighbor performance. HNSW itself is a general proximity-graph method whose quality depends on the indexed metric/data distribution and search parameters. citeturn6view3

Likewise, while the reference class defines a clipped weighted-majority `bundle`, source inspection did not find the reference runtime calling that method. External superposition-capacity literature is therefore useful background but cannot legitimately be retrofitted as the historical justification for `K_WALL`. Established VSA work does show that superposition retrieval becomes crosstalk-limited and that cleanup memory is central to noisy compositional retrieval. citeturn6view4turn7view3

### Recommended terminology

`K_WALL` overstates what has been established. “Wall” naturally suggests a phase transition, mathematical limit or verified safety boundary. None is presently demonstrated.

A better public name would be:

**`K_REF = 307` — reference active-coordinate cap**

or, if the controller must treat it as a maximum:

**`K_MAX_REFERENCE = 307` — calibrated reference occupancy limit**

The phrase **“structural vacuum”** is fine as a representation description—717 of 1024 coordinates are vacuum when exactly 307 remain active—but should not carry thermodynamic implications unless those are separately derived and tested.

### What would elevate 307 from policy to validated engineering point

The strongest next validation is not another verbal argument. It is a preregistered calibration sweep.

Use at least \(K/D=\{0.10,0.15,0.20,\ldots,0.50\}\) plus dense sampling around 0.30, and measure actual target-system objectives: semantic cleanup accuracy, bundle load, same-class/different-class nearest-neighbor margin, false cleanup rate, controller energy/runtime proxies, downstream task success, memory/index cost, and robustness under corruption. Then define an explicit utility function or Pareto criterion *before* selecting the point.

If 307 lies on a distinct Pareto knee or satisfies a stated safety bound that 320 fails, it becomes defensible as an **empirically selected operating limit**. If 288, 307, 320 and 352 are statistically equivalent, the paper should say it is a convenient reference policy.

This is precisely how the HDC/VSA literature treats capacity: in relation to a specified representation, load and retrieval procedure, not as an isolated percentage. Sparse VSA binding can be analyzed formally, and superposition capacity can be quantified, but those model-specific results need to be mapped to Nexus rather than cited by analogy. citeturn6view0turn7view3

# 4. Mathematical analysis of K‑sparse quinary capacity

For exactly \(K\) active coordinates in \(D\) dimensions, with four nonzero values \(\{-2,-1,+1,+2\}\):

\[
N(D,K) = \binom{D}{K} \cdot 4^K.
\]

For occupancy up to \(K_{\mathrm{wall}}\):

\[
N_{\le K}(D,K_{\mathrm{wall}})
=
\sum_{k=0}^{K_{\mathrm{wall}}}
\binom{D}{k} 4^k.
\]

Total unconstrained quinary state space:

\[
N_{\text{full}}(D)
=
5^D.
\]

Information content for fixed \(K\):

\[
I(D,K)
=
\log_2 N(D,K)
=
\log_2 \binom{D}{K}
+
K\log_2 4
=
\log_2 \binom{D}{K}
+
2K.
\]

For large \(D\), Stirling/entropy approximations give:

\[
\log_2 \binom{D}{K}
\approx
D h_2\!\left(\frac{K}{D}\right),
\]

where \(h_2(p)\) is binary entropy in bits.

So:

\[
I(D,K)
\approx
D h_2\!\left(\frac{K}{D}\right)
+
2K.
\]

This is **smooth** in \(K\) for \(0<K<D\). There is **no combinatorial extremum or phase transition specifically at \(K=307\)** for \(D=1024\); capacity simply increases with \(K\) until you approach dense occupancy.

Crucially:

- This **raw state‑space capacity** says nothing by itself about **recoverability** or **collision behavior**.
- It does not single out **30% occupancy** as mathematically special.

# 6. Binding collision analysis and K‑dependence

Reference binding (from HTLA):

\[
(q\otimes r)_i
=
\begin{cases}
0,
& q_i=0 \text{ or } r_i=0,\\[4pt]
\operatorname{sgn}(q_ir_i)\min(|q_i|,|r_i|),
& \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

This is **coordinate‑separable**:

\[
(q\otimes r)_i
=
f(q_i,r_i)
\quad\text{for each }i.
\]

### 6.1 Coordinate‑level ambiguity is independent of global K

For a fixed coordinate \(i\), define:

\[
\operatorname{Poss}(c_i,r_i)
=
\{q_i\in\mathcal Q :
\exists r_i\in\mathcal Q
\text{ with } f(q_i,r_i)=c_i\}.
\]

The size \(|\operatorname{Poss}(c_i,r_i)|\) depends only on:

- the alphabet \(\mathcal Q\),
- the binding rule \(f\),
- the specific values \(c_i,r_i\).

It **does not depend** on how many other coordinates are active elsewhere in the vector.

So, for coordinate‑separable binding with no bundling or global projection:

> **Partial‑unbind ambiguity at coordinate \(i\) is mathematically independent of global occupancy \(K\).**

This directly attacks any claim that **coordinate‑level recoverability** depends on K in this binding model.

### 6.2 Whole‑vector collision probability does depend on K

Even though each coordinate’s ambiguity is independent of K, the **probability that two distinct input pairs produce the same composite vector** increases with:

- the number of active coordinates,
- the per‑coordinate collision probability.

More active coordinates mean more chances for collisions to accumulate into whole‑vector equality.

So:

- **Per‑coordinate information loss** is K‑independent.
- **Whole‑vector collision probability** is K‑dependent.

This could, in principle, motivate an occupancy cap—but the documents you provided **do not show such a derivation or threshold analysis**.

  - incomplete model of the binding process.

- The four states **do not form a simple lattice** purely from \(\operatorname{Poss}\); they need a richer epistemic structure (e.g., information orders, bilattices, or domain‑theoretic partial‑information lattices).

### 3. Mathematical analysis: capacity, overlap, collisions, information

#### 3.1 Combinatorial capacity of K‑sparse quinary vectors

For exactly \(K\) active coordinates in \(D\) dimensions with nonzero values in \(\{-2,-1,+1,+2\}\), the raw state count is:

\[
N(D,K)=\binom{D}{K}4^K.
\]

For occupancy up to \(K_{\text{wall}}\):

\[
N_{\le K}(D,K_{\text{wall}})
=
\sum_{k=0}^{K_{\text{wall}}}
\binom{D}{k}4^k.
\]

The information content is:

\[
\log_2 N(D,K).
\]

Qualitatively:

- **As K increases from 0, \(\log_2 N(D,K)\) increases sharply**, because both the combinatorial term \(\binom{D}{K}\) and the alphabet term \(4^K\) grow.
- **There is no special extremum at \(K\approx 0.3D\)**; the capacity continues to grow until K approaches D, at which point the combinatorial term peaks and then falls if you constrain K.
- **307 is not a combinatorial phase transition**; it’s one point on a smooth curve.

So from pure state‑space size, 307 is **not mathematically privileged**.

#### 3.2 Support‑overlap statistics

For independently drawn K‑sparse supports over D coordinates with uniform support selection, the expected overlap is:

\[
\mathbb E[|A\cap B|]=\frac{K^2}{D}.
\]

At \(D=1024\), \(K=307\):

\[
\mathbb E[|A\cap B|]
=
\frac{307^2}{1024}
\approx
\frac{94{,}249}{1024}
\approx
92.1.
\]

Interpretation:

- Two random 30%‑occupied supports in 1,024‑D share **about 92 active coordinates on average**.

  - \(K=64\): \(\mathbb E[|A\cap B|]\approx 4\).
  - \(K=128\): \(\approx 16\).
  - \(K=256\): \(\approx 64\).
  - \(K=512\): \(\approx 256\).
  - \(K=1024\): \(\approx 1024\) (full overlap).

So 30% occupancy is **moderately sparse** but not extremely sparse. It trades off:

- **Higher raw capacity and per‑vector information** (more active coordinates).
- **Higher expected overlap and collision risk**.

Again, nothing here singles out 307 as a special point; it’s a **design compromise**, not a sharp boundary.

#### 3.3 Binding collision analysis (coordinate vs whole vector)

The reference binding rule (from HTLA):

\[
(q\otimes r)_i
=
\begin{cases}
0,& q_i=0 \text{ or } r_i=0,\\[4pt]
\operatorname{sgn}(q_ir_i)\min(|q_i|,|r_i|),&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Key facts:

- **Coordinate‑separable:** Each coordinate is bound independently; there is no cross‑coordinate mixing.
- **Information‑losing:** The \(\min\) operation collapses multiple input pairs into the same output.

For a fixed coordinate \(i\), given composite \(c_i\) and binder \(r_i\), the possible constituent values are:

\[
\text{Poss}(c_i,r_i)
=
\{q\in\mathcal Q:\exists r\in\mathcal Q\text{ with }q\otimes r=c_i\}.
\]

Crucially:

- **\(\text{Poss}(c_i,r_i)\) depends only on \(c_i\), \(r_i\), the alphabet, and the binding rule.**
- It does **not** depend on how many other coordinates are active elsewhere in the vector.

So:

> **For coordinate‑separable binding, partial‑unbind ambiguity at coordinate \(i\) is independent of global occupancy \(K\).**

What K does affect is:

- **Whole‑vector collision probability:** With more active coordinates, there are more chances for two different logical states to produce the same composite vector.
- **Bundle/superposition behavior:** If you later sum or superpose bound vectors, K affects cross‑talk.

But that’s a **global collision story**, not a coordinate‑level partial‑unbind story. There is no mechanism in the binding rule itself that makes “recoverability suddenly fail past K=307.”

#### 3.4 Information‑theoretic channel view

Treat binding as a channel:

- Random variables \(Q,R\) over \(\mathcal Q\).
- Composite \(C=\operatorname{bind}(Q,R)\).

For a fixed distribution over nonzero vs zero coordinates, you can compute:

- \(H(Q)\), \(H(C)\), \(H(Q\mid C,R)\), \(I(Q;C\mid R)\).

As the probability of zero coordinates changes (i.e., as \(p=K/D\) varies):

- **Per‑coordinate information loss is determined by the binding rule and the distribution over nonzero values**, not by K itself.
- **Global information per vector scales roughly with K**, because you have K active coordinates contributing.

There is no known information‑theoretic result that says:

> “At \(p\approx 0.30\), something qualitatively changes in the binding channel.”

So from this angle too, **30% is not privileged**.

  - Dimensionality D.
  - Sparsity K.
  - Value distribution.
- Very dense vectors can suffer from **hubness** and concentration of measure.
- Very sparse vectors can have **too many near‑orthogonal points**, affecting recall.

But:

- There is **no general result that “30% occupancy in 1,024‑D quinary vectors maximizes ANN separability.”**
- Any such claim would require **actual benchmarks**: recall@k, hubness, contrast vs K.

Without those experiments, we can only say:

- 30% is a **reasonable compromise** between density and sparsity.
- It is **not a proved optimum**.
