This addendum is a research architecture and simulation specification. It is not
an experimentally validated biomedical system, not a clinical treatment design,
and not a wet-lab construction protocol. Statements in the existing Triskelion,
DCSI, BTCA, HTLA, HSL, and related documents may contain heuristics, simulation
assumptions, dimensional errors, unsupported constants, or model-generated
hallucinations. Addendum B explicitly prevents those claims from silently
becoming canonical nanobot mathematics.

SECTION 1: CANONICAL DEFINITION OF "NANOBOT"
================================================================================

1.1 Naming Policy
-----------------
The canonical public-facing term is:

  NANOBOT

This term is retained intentionally because it is concise, memorable, futuristic,
and understandable to a non-specialist audience.

For internal technical precision, the architecture MAY use subtype labels such
as:

  - Biohybrid Nanobot
  - Augmented Biohybrid Nanobot
  - Synthetic Nanobot
  - General-Purpose Synthetic Nanobot

The word "nanobot" in Triskelion does NOT primarily mean a conventionally
miniaturized metal robot containing gears, motors, a silicon CPU, and a miniature
battery.

The intended endpoint is a programmable, cell-like physical agent whose sensing,
state transitions, communication, actuation, metabolism/energy handling, repair,
and swarm behavior are implemented using biological, molecular, synthetic-cell,
and eventually fully engineered mechanisms.

1.2 Distinction from Supporting Microrobotic/Nanorobotic Machinery
------------------------------------------------------------------
Fabricated microrobots, magnetic swimmers, DNA machines, externally actuated
carriers, molecular devices, and related technologies are not rejected by this
architecture.

They are reclassified as part of the NANOBOT TOOLCHAIN.

Their expected roles include:

  - constructing nanobot components;
  - transporting components to assembly sites;
  - manipulating or positioning nanobots;
  - inspecting nanobots or damaged swarms;
  - delivering replacement material;
  - repairing damaged nanobots;
  - removing failed nanobots;
  - providing temporary external actuation;
  - providing diagnostic or calibration support.

Thus:

  SUPPORT MICROROBOTICS != THE NANOBOT

but:

  SUPPORT MICROROBOTICS -> MANUFACTURE + SERVICE + REPAIR OF NANOBOTS

This distinction is canonical for Addendum B.

SECTION 2: DEVELOPMENT ROADMAP - BIOHYBRID FIRST, SYNTHETIC ENDPOINT
================================================================================

2.1 Architectural Strategy
--------------------------
The preferred development direction is:

  PHASE 1: BIOHYBRID NANOBOT
      Existing living cell supplies the biological runtime.
      Synthetic control layers supply increasing programmability.

  PHASE 2: AUGMENTED BIOHYBRID NANOBOT
      Synthetic sensing, communication, logic, safety, identity, and external
      control are progressively expanded while dependence on native cell
      behavior is reduced.

  PHASE 3: MINIMAL SYNTHETIC NANOBOT
      A synthetic cell-like chassis provides bounded sensing, signaling, energy
      handling, local state, limited actuation, and communication.

  PHASE 4: AUTONOMOUS SYNTHETIC NANOBOT
      The synthetic chassis gains robust navigation, adaptive task behavior,
      self-diagnostics, bounded self-repair, and swarm-level participation.

  PHASE 5: GENERAL-PURPOSE SYNTHETIC NANOBOT PLATFORM
      A common chassis and runtime execute task-specific programs without
      requiring organ-specific nanobot species.

2.2 Why the Biohybrid Stage Exists
----------------------------------
The biohybrid stage is a bootstrap substrate.

A living cell already contains extremely sophisticated machinery for:

  - membrane maintenance;
  - molecular sensing;
  - protein synthesis;
  - energy conversion;
  - intracellular transport;
  - environmental response;
  - motility in some cell types;
  - repair;
  - secretion;
  - regulated state transitions.

The near-term engineering problem is therefore not to recreate all cellular
machinery immediately. It is to impose a reliable programmable control and
safety layer over a biological runtime while learning which functions can later
be replaced by synthetic equivalents.

2.3 Why the Fully Synthetic Endpoint Remains Preferred
------------------------------------------------------
The long-term target remains a fully synthetic architecture because it offers the
possibility of:

  - a reduced and explicitly defined state space;
  - tighter control of permissible operations;
  - explicit communication protocols;
  - explicit memory models;
  - reproducible component interfaces;
  - substrate-level observability;
  - reduced dependence on undocumented native cell behavior;
  - common swarm semantics;
  - stronger compatibility with distributed-computing principles.

The synthetic endpoint must NOT be described as deterministic merely because it
is engineered. At microscopic biological and molecular scales, stochasticity,
thermal noise, diffusion, degradation, fluid dynamics, communication loss,
component failure, and environmental uncertainty remain fundamental system
properties.

SECTION 3: ONE GENERAL-PURPOSE SWARM, NOT ORGAN-SPECIFIC BOT SPECIES
================================================================================

3.1 Rejected Architecture: Organ-Specific Nanobots
--------------------------------------------------
The following architecture is explicitly rejected as the default design:

  LiverBot
  HeartBot
  KidneyBot
  LungBot
  BrainBot
  ...

A proliferation of organ-specific nanobot species would create avoidable
complexity in:

  - manufacturing;
  - fleet versioning;
  - safety certification;
  - compatibility;
  - command routing;
  - lifecycle management;
  - repair tooling;
  - in-body population accounting;
  - failure analysis;
  - long-term maintenance.

It would also increase the number of biologically active variants capable of
misclassification, ectopic activity, inappropriate proliferation, or other
pathological behavior.

3.2 Canonical Architecture: General-Purpose Chassis
---------------------------------------------------
The preferred design is a single general-purpose nanobot platform with a common:

  - physical chassis;
  - identity system;
  - safety kernel;
  - communication protocol;
  - local sensing interface;
  - task interpreter;
  - resource/energy model;
  - integrity monitor;
  - swarm protocol;
  - shutdown path.

Task specialization is temporary and programmatic.

Conceptually:

  GENERAL-PURPOSE NANOBOT
          |
          +--> receives authorized objective
          |
          +--> observes local environment
          |
          +--> verifies that local conditions match objective
          |
          +--> loads/activates bounded task state
          |
          +--> performs repair/diagnostic/transport/signaling work
          |
          +--> exits task state
          |
          +--> returns to idle, safe, maintenance, or disposal state

A liver repair mission and a cardiac repair mission SHOULD therefore use the same
base nanobot population whenever physically possible. The difference is the
mission state, local biological context, and authorized action set, not a new
species of nanobot.

================================================================================
SECTION 4: THE SWARM IS THE COMPUTER
================================================================================

4.1 Individual Nanobots Should Remain Locally Simple
----------------------------------------------------
The architecture does not require each nanobot to contain a miniature general
computer. The individual agent may remain comparatively simple.

The minimal conceptual local capabilities are:

  - sense;
  - classify local state;
  - maintain bounded internal state;
  - compare observed state with expected/authorized state;
  - exchange small messages;
  - adjust local action;
  - maintain energy/resource accounting;
  - detect local integrity failure;
  - enter a safe state;
  - participate in local swarm consensus.

Complex behavior is expected to emerge from interaction among many restricted
agents.

4.2 DCSI Is the Computational Skeleton
--------------------------------------
The existing Distributed Cognitive Swarm Intelligence (DCSI) work already
contains architectural primitives directly relevant to the nanobot system:

  - time-varying neighborhood graphs;
  - partial observability;
  - stochastic node behavior;
  - strict local-only information access;
  - asynchronous local clocks;
  - bounded communication;
  - bounded compute;
  - bounded memory;
  - resource depletion;
  - sensor noise;
  - packet loss/corruption;
  - node failure;
  - catastrophic lesion tests;
  - re-coherence after node loss;
  - emergent consensus;
  - salience propagation;
  - module-aware fault tolerance.

Therefore, nanobot research begins by REUSING and AUDITING DCSI, not by inventing
a new distributed-systems theory from zero.

4.3 BTCA Is the Biological Mapping Layer
----------------------------------------
The Biological Tri-Cell Architecture provides an existing biological abstraction
for:

  - Nucleus/Core-A: identity and slow regulation;
  - Cytoplasm/Core-B: processing, prediction, metabolism, and stress integration;
  - Membrane/Core-C: sensing, receptors, communication, and environmental
    interface;
  - local metabolic limits;
  - local signaling;
  - lesion recovery;
  - mitosis/apoptosis state handling;
  - contact/density regulation;
  - local Lyapunov-style homeostatic control.

BTCA is therefore treated as a candidate BIOLOGICAL ADAPTER for the early
biohybrid nanobot, not as unquestioned ground truth.

================================================================================
SECTION 5: CONTROL PLANE - AGI OBJECTIVES PLUS LOCAL BIOLOGICAL REALITY
================================================================================

5.1 External AGI/Server Role
----------------------------
The external AGI is the high-level control plane.

Its responsibilities may include:

  - interpreting patient/system objectives;
  - selecting mission goals;
  - assigning priority;
  - deciding whether a repair task is authorized;
  - monitoring aggregate telemetry;
  - issuing coarse task instructions;
  - revoking tasks;
  - ordering swarm withdrawal, quiescence, or disposal;
  - coordinating external imaging, actuation, or service microrobots.

The AGI MUST NOT be treated as omniscient.

The body remains a partially observed environment. A remote model can be wrong,
outdated, spatially imprecise, or operating on delayed telemetry.

5.2 Local Verification Rule
---------------------------
External instruction is NECESSARY but SHOULD NOT be SUFFICIENT for high-risk local
action.

The canonical rule is:

  EXTERNAL INTENT + LOCAL VERIFICATION + SAFETY AUTHORITY -> ACTION

A command such as "repair tissue in region X" should be interpreted as an
authorization envelope, not as unconditional permission to proliferate or alter
tissue.

A nanobot/swarm should independently determine whether local sensed conditions
match the expected tissue/damage context before activating high-impact behavior.

5.3 Capability Is Not Authority
-------------------------------
A central architectural invariant is:

  CAPABILITY != AUTHORITY

A general-purpose nanobot may physically possess mechanisms that could support
multiple tasks, but it must not have standing permission to activate all of them.

The most privileged capabilities include, at minimum:

  - replication;
  - differentiation/state conversion;
  - persistent engraftment;
  - tissue remodeling;
  - long-lived secretion of growth signals;
  - irreversible local structural change;
  - transfer of persistent program state to other cells/agents.

These operations require stronger authorization and stronger local verification
than ordinary sensing, messaging, or reversible maintenance activity.

================================================================================
SECTION 6: GENERAL-PURPOSE REPAIR MODEL
================================================================================

6.1 Tissue Repair Is a Mission, Not a Nanobot Species
-----------------------------------------------------
A repair mission is represented conceptually as a temporary task profile loaded
onto the common nanobot chassis.

The task profile may specify:

  - target condition class;
  - expected local biomarkers/physical features;
  - allowed action set;
  - forbidden action set;
  - maximum duration;
  - maximum resource expenditure;
  - maximum local swarm density;
  - maximum replication authority, normally zero unless explicitly enabled;
  - success criteria;
  - abort criteria;
  - disposal/quiescence behavior.

6.2 Navigation Does Not Require a Microscopic GPS
-------------------------------------------------
The original concept remains: nanobots need not maintain a complete internal map
of the body.

Navigation can be formulated as a hierarchy of cues:

  1. External coarse guidance from the control plane.
  2. Flow-assisted transport through the body.
  3. Local chemical/biophysical gradients.
  4. Receptor-defined tissue context.
  5. Local damage/inflammation/structural cues.
  6. Swarm-to-swarm/local-neighbor signaling.
  7. Final local verification before task activation.

The exact navigation mathematics are NOT canonical in this addendum. Earlier
conversation-level chemotaxis equations are explicitly classified as illustrative
toy models pending the audit defined in Section 11.

6.3 Stem-Cell-Like Repair Versus Permanent Stem-Cell Identity
-------------------------------------------------------------
The architecture does not require every nanobot to permanently be a pluripotent
stem cell.

The biohybrid stage may use living cells because natural cells provide useful
metabolism, sensing, synthesis, and repair machinery. However, unrestricted
pluripotency and unrestricted proliferation are safety liabilities.

The preferred abstraction is:

  programmable regenerative behavior

rather than:

  permanently free-roaming pluripotent stem cell

The fully synthetic endpoint should reproduce only the capabilities needed for a
mission, not every behavior of a natural stem cell.

================================================================================
SECTION 7: THE CENTRAL SAFETY PROBLEM - PREVENTING PATHOLOGICAL GROWTH
================================================================================

7.1 Terminology
---------------
The term "metastasis" is used colloquially in project discussion to describe a
swarm or engineered-cell population spreading and growing where it should not.

For technical work, the failure classes must be distinguished:

  - uncontrolled proliferation;
  - tumorigenicity;
  - neoplastic transformation;
  - teratoma formation from residual pluripotent cells;
  - ectopic engraftment;
  - inappropriate differentiation;
  - unwanted migration/dissemination;
  - escape from apoptosis;
  - mutation or silencing of safety circuitry;
  - pathological growth-factor signaling;
  - command/control loss;
  - swarm-state corruption.

7.2 Why General Purpose Increases the Safety Burden
---------------------------------------------------
A general-purpose chassis is desirable because it reduces fleet complexity.
However, generality also increases the number of actions a compromised agent may
be physically capable of performing.

Therefore the system must enforce separation between:

  - what the chassis CAN do;
  - what the current mission MAY do;
  - what local conditions justify;
  - what the safety kernel will permit.

7.3 Defense in Depth
--------------------
No single anti-proliferation mechanism is sufficient.

The research architecture should investigate independent containment layers such
as:

  - bounded replication authority;
  - density/contact-dependent inhibition;
  - resource-dependent replication limits;
  - finite mission lifetime;
  - integrity-dependent task execution;
  - independent apoptosis/disposal mechanisms;
  - local tissue-context verification;
  - external revocation;
  - swarm quorum requirements for privileged actions;
  - anomaly detection for lineage/state drift;
  - population caps;
  - replication accounting;
  - fail-closed behavior after communication loss;
  - non-replicating fallback modes;
  - removal by immune-compatible or external service mechanisms.

These are architectural research requirements, not claims that present biology
can implement them perfectly.

7.4 Safety Circuits Must Be Treated as Fallible
-----------------------------------------------
A kill switch, apoptosis trigger, contact-inhibition rule, or other containment
mechanism must never be modeled as infallible.

The simulation program must include failure modes such as:

  - switch mutation;
  - switch silencing;
  - receptor loss;
  - false-negative context sensing;
  - false-positive repair activation;
  - signal spoofing/corruption;
  - local resource-model failure;
  - partial controller isolation;
  - multiple simultaneous safety-layer failures.

The safety question is therefore not:

  "Does the system have a kill switch?"

It is:

  "What is the probability and consequence of escape when one or more independent
   containment layers fail?"

SECTION 8: NANOBOT INTERNAL ARCHITECTURE - LOGICAL TARGET
================================================================================

8.1 Common Logical Modules
--------------------------
The long-term general-purpose synthetic nanobot should be designed around common
logical modules even if their physical implementation changes across generations:

  1. Identity and Integrity Core
     - persistent identity;
     - version/provenance;
     - integrity checks;
     - lineage/task authorization state.

  2. Sensor Interface
     - local chemical cues;
     - local physical cues;
     - receptor/ligand state;
     - damage/inflammation context;
     - neighbor-derived state summaries.

  3. Local State and Task Engine
     - bounded state machine;
     - task authorization;
     - local context verification;
     - abort/rollback state.

  4. Communication Interface
     - constrained local messages;
     - swarm summaries;
     - external-control interface where physically possible;
     - authentication/integrity appropriate to the substrate.

  5. Energy/Resource Controller
     - energy acquisition;
     - energy accounting;
     - task-energy limits;
     - starvation response.

  6. Actuation/Repair Interface
     - movement or adhesion change;
     - material transport;
     - local secretion/deposition;
     - removal of debris;
     - structural repair functions;
     - other mission-defined outputs.

  7. Safety Kernel
     - privileged-operation gating;
     - density/population constraints;
     - integrity response;
     - shutdown/quiescence;
     - apoptosis/disassembly where substrate permits.

  8. Maintenance Interface
     - external microrobot/DNA-machine interaction;
     - component replacement;
     - calibration;
     - retrieval/removal.

8.2 Substrate Independence
--------------------------
The logical architecture should remain stable while the physical substrate
changes:

  GENERATION 1:
    Logical Nanobot Runtime -> Biological Adapter -> Living Cell

  GENERATION 2:
    Logical Nanobot Runtime -> Hybrid Molecular Adapter -> Augmented Living Cell

  GENERATION 3+:
    Logical Nanobot Runtime -> Synthetic Molecular Runtime -> Synthetic Chassis

This is intentionally aligned with the broader Nexus principle of preserving
functional organization while allowing the implementation substrate to change.

SECTION 9: SUPPORT MICROROBOTS, DNA MACHINES, AND EXTERNAL SERVICE SYSTEMS
================================================================================

9.1 Service Layer
-----------------
Conventional microrobotic and molecular-machine research is integrated as a
service layer surrounding the nanobot ecosystem.

Candidate service roles include:

  - assembly jig;
  - component shuttle;
  - magnetic positioning aid;
  - targeted delivery carrier;
  - diagnostic probe;
  - nanobot retriever;
  - damaged-agent remover;
  - localized energy/actuation aid;
  - sensor/calibration device;
  - repair-material carrier.

9.2 Separation of Responsibilities
----------------------------------
The Triskelion nanobot should not be forced to contain every possible physical
function internally.

Where a function is rare, expensive, bulky, or better performed externally, it
may be delegated to a service device.

This creates an ecosystem analogous to conventional computing:

  NANOBOT             = endpoint/node
  SWARM               = distributed computer
  AGI/SERVER           = control plane
  SERVICE MICROROBOTS  = maintenance/field-service equipment
  DNA/MOLECULAR TOOLS  = molecular fabrication/repair equipment
  EXTERNAL IMAGING     = observability infrastructure

SECTION 10: RELATIONSHIP TO EXISTING TRISKELION FAILURE CONTINGENCIES
================================================================================

10.1 Existing Contingencies Are Reused
--------------------------------------
The Triskelion/DCSI/BTCA corpus already models several classes of non-determinism
and damage relevant to nanobots:

  - stochastic node failure;
  - bit/message corruption;
  - sensor noise;
  - environmental drift;
  - asynchronous clocks;
  - bounded neighbor counts;
  - bounded communication;
  - bounded energy;
  - catastrophic lesion events;
  - re-coherence after node loss;
  - local-only consensus;
  - module failure;
  - identity drift;
  - packet storms/silence;
  - isolation;
  - runaway proliferation in the BTCA pathology model;
  - apoptosis and density/contact gating;
  - local homeostatic control.

Nanobot design should extend these contingencies rather than duplicating them.

10.2 New Nanobot-Specific Failure Classes
-----------------------------------------
The nanobot layer adds or emphasizes:

  - ectopic task execution;
  - tumorigenicity and uncontrolled proliferation;
  - task-program corruption;
  - AGI command error;
  - AGI/swarm disagreement;
  - local-context misclassification;
  - safety-circuit escape;
  - persistence beyond authorized lifetime;
  - loss of external controllability;
  - inappropriate material deposition;
  - inappropriate tissue remodeling;
  - service-tool induced damage;
  - hybrid-to-synthetic interface mismatch;
  - repair operations that accidentally preserve a compromised nanobot.

SECTION 11: MANDATORY DCSI + BTCA MATHEMATICS AND CHEMISTRY AUDIT
================================================================================

11.1 Audit Gate
---------------
BEFORE constructing or canonizing the actual nanobot mathematics, the existing
DCSI, BTCA, Triskelion Master Specification, and Addendum A equations MUST be
audited line by line.

No equation may be inherited merely because it appears in an older master
specification, a NotebookLM/Gemini-generated synthesis, a podcast, a model answer,
or an earlier research document.

11.2 Required Classification Labels
------------------------------------
Every equation, numerical constant, kinetic rule, scaling law, and claimed
physical constraint must receive one or more of the following labels:

  [DERIVED]
    Follows from stated assumptions by an explicit derivation that can be checked.

  [BIOLOGICALLY GROUNDED]
    Form and/or parameterization is supported by identifiable experimental or
    peer-reviewed biological literature for the relevant substrate and regime.

  [PHYSICALLY GROUNDED]
    Form and/or parameterization follows from established physical law in the
    relevant scale/regime.

  [SIMULATION ASSUMPTION]
    Deliberately imposed to define the experiment; not claimed as a law of nature.

  [HEURISTIC]
    Useful engineering/controller approximation without a formal derivation or
    direct biological equivalence.

  [DIMENSIONALLY SUSPECT]
    Units do not obviously close, variables mix counts/concentrations/probabilities,
    or the equation requires missing dimensional factors.

  [UNSUPPORTED]
    No adequate source, derivation, experimental result, or stated modeling reason
    currently supports the claim.

  [CONTRADICTORY]
    Conflicts with another canonical constraint or equation.

  [TERMINOLOGY ERROR]
    Mathematical, chemical, biological, or physical terminology is used in a way
    that changes or obscures its technical meaning.

  [UNKNOWN]
    Status cannot yet be determined and requires targeted research.

11.3 Required Audit Record for Every Equation
---------------------------------------------
For every equation E_j, the audit must record:

  Equation ID:
  Source filename:
  Source section/line/page:
  Original equation:
  Intended meaning:
  Variables:
  Units for every variable:
  Domain/range:
  Time/space scale:
  Derivation or source:
  Classification label(s):
  Dimensional analysis:
  Hidden assumptions:
  Known failure modes:
  Numerical implementation concerns:
  Whether the equation is local or observer-side:
  Whether a nanobot can physically access every required variable:
  Whether the equation violates bandwidth/memory/energy constraints:
  Falsification or test method:
  Recommended action: KEEP / REWRITE / REPARAMETERIZE / QUARANTINE / DELETE

11.5 Preliminary Audit Flags Already Identified
-----------------------------------------------
The following items are NOT a completed audit. They are known issues that must be
entered into the formal audit queue.

A. RECEPTOR-BINDING DIMENSIONALITY

Current form appearing in the BTCA/master material:

  p_bind = 1 - exp(-k_on * R_free * dt)

If k_on is expressed as a conventional bimolecular association constant in
concentration^-1 time^-1, multiplying it directly by a receptor COUNT and time
does not produce a dimensionless exponent. The model must explicitly distinguish
receptor count, receptor surface density, ligand concentration, encounter rate,
and pseudo-first-order approximations.

Status:
  [DIMENSIONALLY SUSPECT]
  [REQUIRES REWRITE]

B. 32-NT RNA == 64 RAW BITS

The mapping:

  4 nucleotide symbols -> 2 raw bits per nucleotide
  32 nucleotides -> 64 raw theoretical bits

is an information-capacity abstraction. It is not automatically equivalent to 64
reliable application bits after sequence constraints, coding redundancy,
recognition requirements, degradation, framing, error correction, and biological
implementation overhead.

The term "32-bp RNA" is also incorrect unless a duplex is specifically intended.
A single RNA sequence should normally be described as 32 nt.

Status:
  raw mapping: [DERIVED]
  reliable biological payload claim: [UNSUPPORTED] until channel model exists
  "32-bp RNA": [TERMINOLOGY ERROR] where single-stranded RNA is intended

C. ADDENDUM A RNA SCRAMBLE SIDE-CHANNEL PROBLEM

The current Addendum A transcoder returns both:

  rna_str
  scramble_depth

but the strict 32-nt/64-bit channel does not visibly encode scramble_depth inside
the same payload. A receiver therefore requires side information to reverse the
scrambling, violating the claim of a self-contained strict 64-bit channel unless a
new in-band coding scheme is defined.

Status:
  [CONTRADICTORY]
  [REQUIRES REWRITE]

D. CONNECTIVITY/PERCOLATION CONSTANT

The DCSI material currently uses a constant-style condition resembling:

  pi * R_c^2 * N / A > 4.5

and describes it as ensuring connectivity.

A constant mean-degree/percolation threshold is not generally identical to a full
connectivity guarantee for a random geometric graph. Connectivity, giant-component
formation, k-connectivity, boundary effects, dimensionality, and post-lesion
connectivity must be separated.

Status:
  [HEURISTIC] or [SIMULATION ASSUMPTION] unless properly derived
  [UNSUPPORTED] as a universal connectivity guarantee

E. XOR USED AS "REINFORCEMENT"

XOR can be useful as a binary binding/difference operation, but XOR by itself does
not mean consensus reinforcement. Consensus/bundling and binding must be separated.
Majority/bundling, weighted accumulation, Hamming similarity, and XOR binding
should not be conflated.

Status:
  [TERMINOLOGY/OPERATOR ERROR]
  [REQUIRES REWRITE]

F. HSL DISTANCE/METRIC CLAIM

A composite expression combining physical distance, hypervector similarity, and
salience difference is not automatically a valid Riemannian metric. Normalization,
metric properties, triangle inequality, topology, and manifold assumptions must be
proved or the quantity should be called a dissimilarity/energy functional.

Status:
  [UNSUPPORTED] as a formal Riemannian metric until proven
  [HEURISTIC] as a simulation dissimilarity if explicitly declared

G. CHEMOTAXIS/NAVIGATION EQUATIONS FROM CURRENT DISCUSSION

Any previously proposed single-gradient equation of the form:

  velocity = gradient response + flow + noise

is a toy intuition only. It omits receptor adaptation/saturation, adhesion,
extravasation, stochastic persistence, geometry, tissue mechanics, multi-cue
integration, and other factors.

Status:
  [HEURISTIC]
  [NON-CANONICAL]

H. ONE VESICLE PER CYCLE VS "INCREASE EMISSION RATE"

The master/BTCA materials simultaneously state a strict one-vesicle-per-cycle
communication bottleneck and describe lesion-boundary cells as increasing vesicle
emission rate.

The model must specify whether "increase" means:

  - shorten the biological cycle;
  - increase payload priority;
  - increase release probability;
  - emit more than one vesicle, thereby violating the constraint;
  - or something else.

Status:
  [CONTRADICTORY]

I. "HILL" FUNCTION WITHOUT HILL EXPONENT

Addendum A uses a rectified saturating function resembling:

  H(z;k) = max(0,z) / (max(0,z) + k)

This is a saturating response with an implicit exponent of 1, not a general Hill
function unless the modeling decision is explicitly stated. Claims of biological
validation require parameter-specific sources.

Status:
  [HEURISTIC] unless sourced and parameterized
  [TERMINOLOGY CLARIFICATION REQUIRED]

J. BIOLOGICAL CONSTANT TRANSFER ACROSS SUBSTRATES

Constants or energy budgets borrowed from one organism or cell type must not be
silently transferred to mammalian cells, engineered cells, synthetic cells, or
future nanobots. Values such as power budgets, receptor rates, diffusion ranges,
and ATP costs must be tied to the relevant substrate.

Status:
  [UNSUPPORTED] when cross-substrate transfer lacks justification

K. PHI-PROXY STATUS

The existing Phi-proxy is an engineering observer/controller metric unless a
separate derivation demonstrates equivalence to a specific formal theory. It must
not be presented as direct proof or measurement of consciousness.

Status:
  [HEURISTIC]
  [OBSERVER-SIDE METRIC]

L. JL / SIMHASH / COLLISION CLAIMS IN ADDENDUM A

The Johnson-Lindenstrauss, SimHash, collision, and projection sections require
fresh mathematical review. In particular:

  - constants in JL bounds are theorem/assumption dependent;
  - projected dimension should not casually be described as "bits" when the
    mathematical object is a real-valued embedding dimension;
  - exact sketch equality probability is not the same as false consensus under a
    similarity threshold;
  - nearest-neighbor dictionary effects require a proper multiple-comparison model;
  - a low raw collision probability does not by itself "validate" semantic
    transmission.

M. RIEMANNIAN CENTER-OF-MASS ASSUMPTIONS

The Addendum A Riemannian mean code appears to operate on normalized vectors on a
unit hypersphere. That does not automatically implement a general HSL manifold.
The manifold, metric, normalization, curvature assumptions, and local-vs-global
availability must be explicit.

Status:
  [SIMULATION ASSUMPTION] if restricted to a hypersphere model
  [UNSUPPORTED] as a universal HSL implementation

N. GLOBAL VARIABLES INSIDE A LOCAL-ONLY ARCHITECTURE

Any equation requiring a global field, global mean, global center, or all-node
state must be classified as either:

  - observer-side telemetry;
  - an offline analysis quantity;
  - or a distributed approximation computed from local messages.

A nanobot cannot use a variable it cannot physically observe or reconstruct.

11.6 Audit Rule for Numerical Constants
---------------------------------------
Every numerical constant must answer:

  WHY THIS NUMBER?

Permitted answers include:

  - experimentally measured value for the relevant substrate;
  - derived physical constant;
  - value chosen by dimensional scaling;
  - parameter sweep range;
  - explicit simulation stress-test value;
  - fitted parameter with dataset/procedure;
  - placeholder marked UNKNOWN.

"Research validated" without a traceable source is not an acceptable provenance
label.

11.7 Audit Rule for Units
-------------------------
Every physical equation must pass dimensional analysis before implementation.

Counts, concentrations, probabilities, rates, energies, powers, lengths, times,
velocities, diffusion coefficients, receptor densities, and dimensionless control
states must not be mixed without explicit conversion.

11.8 Audit Rule for Accessibility
---------------------------------
Every state variable used by a nanobot controller must be classified as:

  LOCAL SENSOR VALUE
  LOCAL INTERNAL VALUE
  NEIGHBOR-DERIVED VALUE
  EXTERNAL COMMAND VALUE
  OBSERVER-ONLY VALUE

Observer-only values are forbidden inside the nanobot control loop.

SECTION 12: ONLY AFTER THE AUDIT - CONSTRUCT THE ACTUAL NANOBOT MATHEMATICS
================================================================================

12.1 No Premature Canonical Equations
-------------------------------------
Addendum B intentionally does NOT declare a final nanobot state vector,
chemotaxis law, reaction network, population-growth law, or safety equation.

Those equations are to be constructed AFTER completion of the audit in Section
11.

12.2 Required Mathematical Layers
---------------------------------
The post-audit nanobot mathematics must be built in the following order:

  LAYER 1: PHYSICAL SUBSTRATE MODEL
    geometry, fluid/tissue environment, diffusion, temperature, mechanics,
    degradation, transport.

  LAYER 2: SINGLE-NANOBOT STATE MODEL
    identity, integrity, local sensor state, task state, energy/resources,
    communication state, safety state.

  LAYER 3: SENSOR AND RECEPTOR MODEL
    concentrations vs counts, binding kinetics, saturation, noise, adaptation,
    false positives/negatives.

  LAYER 4: MOTION/NAVIGATION MODEL
    flow, active motility if present, adhesion, local gradients, persistence,
    boundary crossing, uncertainty.

  LAYER 5: COMMUNICATION MODEL
    channel substrate, effective payload, error model, latency, local reach,
    collision/saturation, authentication/integrity.

  LAYER 6: SWARM TOPOLOGY AND CONSENSUS
    time-varying graph, local neighborhoods, failure tolerance, re-coherence,
    no hidden global access.

  LAYER 7: TASK EXECUTION MODEL
    reversible tasks first; privileged irreversible actions separately.

  LAYER 8: POPULATION/REPLICATION MODEL
    replication authority, density limits, resource limits, mutation/escape,
    apoptosis/disassembly, tumorigenicity stress tests.

  LAYER 9: AGI CONTROL-PLANE MODEL
    command latency, incomplete observability, command error, revocation,
    disagreement with local state.

  LAYER 10: SERVICE/MAINTENANCE MODEL
    interaction with support microrobots, molecular tools, retrieval, repair,
    and replacement.

12.3 Acceptance Criteria for a New Equation
-------------------------------------------
No new nanobot equation becomes canonical until it has:

  - defined variables;
  - defined units;
  - defined domain/range;
  - stated assumptions;
  - source or derivation;
  - classification label;
  - dimensional closure;
  - numerical stability analysis;
  - implementation mapping;
  - falsification test;
  - failure-mode analysis;
  - confirmation that all required inputs are physically accessible.

13.3 Phase N2 - Pathology and Escape Simulation
-----------------------------------------------
Stress tests must include:

  - unauthorized replication;
  - false tissue classification;
  - corrupted AGI command;
  - external communication loss;
  - apoptosis failure;
  - density-gating failure;
  - mutation/silencing of one safety layer;
  - mutation/silencing of multiple safety layers;
  - ectopic migration;
  - over-repair;
  - pathological tissue remodeling;
  - runaway local population growth;
  - swarm fragmentation;
  - recovery after large node loss.

13.4 Phase N3 - Biohybrid Mapping
---------------------------------
Only after N0-N2 produce a stable abstract architecture should candidate living
cell substrates be mapped onto the logical modules.

The mapping must document which functions are:

  - native biological behavior;
  - engineered control behavior;
  - external service behavior;
  - simulated abstraction only.

13.5 Phase N4 - Progressive Synthetic Replacement
--------------------------------------------------
Replace biological functions one subsystem at a time while preserving the common
logical interfaces.

The migration principle is:

  DO NOT REWRITE THE SWARM ARCHITECTURE FOR EACH NEW SUBSTRATE.

Instead:

  preserve the logical protocol;
  replace the substrate adapter;
  re-audit the physical model;
  revalidate safety.

 10. How can support microrobots and molecular tools inspect, repair, retrieve,

 11. Which functions are best left biological in the hybrid generations, and
     which should be replaced first by synthetic components?

 12. What mathematical invariants must remain unchanged when migrating from a
     living-cell substrate to a fully synthetic chassis?

 13. What constitutes successful repair without over-repair?

 14. How should the system prove mission completion and return to a safe state?

 15. What is the maximum safe in-body population under different communication,
     energy, immune, and clearance regimes?

SECTION 15: CANONICAL DECISIONS RECORDED BY ADDENDUM B
================================================================================

The following decisions are canonical unless superseded by later Triskelion
review:

  1. The public project term is NANOBOT.

  2. Triskelion nanobots are not defined as miniature conventional metal robots.

  3. Fabricated microrobots, magnetic swimmers, DNA machines, molecular devices,
     and externally actuated carriers belong to the fabrication, service, and
     repair toolchain surrounding the actual nanobots.

  4. The development path begins with biohybrid programmable cellular agents and
     deliberately advances toward a fully synthetic nanobot chassis.

  5. The final platform is general purpose. Organ-specific permanent nanobot
     species are rejected as the default architecture.

  6. Task specialization is programmatic and temporary.

  7. The swarm, not the individual nanobot, is the primary distributed computer.

  8. DCSI is the initial computational skeleton and BTCA is the initial biological
     mapping layer, both subject to audit.

  9. The AGI/server is a control plane, not an omniscient controller.

 10. High-risk actions require both external authorization and local contextual
     verification.

 11. Capability is explicitly separated from authority.

 12. Replication, persistent engraftment, differentiation/state conversion, and
     irreversible remodeling are privileged operations.

 13. Preventing uncontrolled proliferation, ectopic persistence, safety-circuit
     escape, and other cancer-like/pathological behaviors is a first-class
     architectural problem, not a late-stage implementation detail.

 14. Existing Triskelion stochastic, lesion, failure, and homeostatic contingencies
     are inherited conceptually where valid, not reinvented.

 15. No current DCSI/BTCA mathematical or chemical equation is exempt from the
     Section 11 audit merely because it appears in the existing master document.

 16. The actual canonical nanobot mathematics will be constructed only after the
     audit classifies, repairs, or rejects the inherited equations.
