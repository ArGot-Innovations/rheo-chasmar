## Mission, and the one thing that has to change first

This material's real subject is a genuine, serious, still-open question: what, if anything, would identity/continuity require under a hypothetical substrate transfer? That question has an actual academic home — serious philosophers and technical researchers have been working on it for decades. The current draft is not that paper. It's an operational research protocol — IRB checklists, IRB-style informed consent language covering "destruction," specific instrument calibration schedules, and a Bayesian posterior threshold (">0.999") that gates authorization to proceed with "destructive transfer" — built on top of a measurement technique (QHRF: Brillouin microscopy, NV-diamond magnetometry) that has no established scientific connection to the thing it claims to measure.

That's the load-bearing problem, and it's not fixable by better citations or tighter math, because there's no signal under the threshold to calibrate against. A Bayesian decision procedure needs a likelihood function connecting the measurement to the hypothesis; "P(identity preserved | Brillouin spectrum)" has never been established to mean anything at all. Presenting a specific numeric go/no-go threshold for an irreversible act creates false actionability regardless of how far in the future it's framed as occurring. This is the same category of error as every fabricated number already corrected in the HTLA and HTNLA rewrites — a precise-looking number standing in for a real derivation — except here the number would gate something that can't be undone. The fix is the same principle applied at larger scale: the operational protocol comes out entirely; the genuine philosophical and technical question stays, gets a real literature review, and gets written as what it actually is.

**This document's genre after rewrite: serious speculative philosophy / technology futurism**, in the tradition of the real academic and quasi-academic literature on this topic (below) — not an experimental protocol, not medical or research-ethics guidance, not a near-term engineering plan.

## Real literature to build the rewrite on

- **Sandberg, A. & Bostrom, N. (2008). *Whole Brain Emulation: A Roadmap*, Technical Report #2008-3, Future of Humanity Institute, University of Oxford.** The structural model for this entire paper: systematically separates what's required (scanning resolution, translation to functional models, computational infrastructure) from what's known, and gives calibrated, probabilistic timelines (~50% probability of WBE by 2064) instead of phase-1/phase-2/phase-3 month counts.
- **A 2025 follow-up state-of-the-field assessment (arXiv:2510.15745, "State of Brain Emulation Report 2025")** for current, honestly-calibrated numbers: recording technology has reached roughly a million neurons via calcium imaging in mice, nowhere near full-human-brain scale. Use this to replace the draft's timeline entirely.
- **Parfit, D. (1984). *Reasons and Persons*, Oxford: Clarendon Press.** The canonical philosophical treatment of personal identity and teletransportation — the origin of "identity is not what matters," which directly bears on whether a transferred pattern would *be* the original person or merely resemble them, and whether that distinction is even the right question to ask.
- **Chalmers, D. J. (2010). "The Singularity: A Philosophical Analysis," *Journal of Consciousness Studies*, 17(9–10), 7–65**, and **Chalmers, D. (1995). "Absent qualia, fading qualia, dancing qualia," in Metzinger (ed.), *Conscious Experience*.** Chalmers explicitly separates five distinct questions any uploading/transfer proposal conflates: would the result behave like the original, have its memories, be conscious, be numerically the same person, and be "as good as survival"? Use this decomposition directly in place of the draft's single composite fidelity score. Chalmers also argues for *gradual* replacement over destructive scan-and-transfer specifically on continuity grounds — directly relevant context for why the draft's single-shot "destructive transfer" framing is the philosophically weakest version of this idea, not a refinement of it.
- **Bamford, S. & Danaher, J. (2017). "Transfer of personality to a synthetic human ('mind uploading') and the social construction of identity," *Journal of Consciousness Studies*, 24(11–12), 6–30.**
- **Hanson, R. (2016). *The Age of Em*, Oxford University Press** — the real economics/social-implications treatment of a world with brain emulations, useful for any speculative-society material worth keeping, without implying the underlying technology exists.
- **Randal Koene's "substrate-independent minds" (SIM) framing (carboncopies.org)** for the real terminology this space actually uses, as an alternative to "phenomenal continuity" language borrowed without attribution.
- **The PhilPapers 2009 survey data on personal identity** (34% psychological-continuity, 17% biological, 12% further-fact view among professional philosophers; 38% judged teletransportation to be survival) — cite directly as evidence this is a genuinely disputed question among experts, which is the honest framing the rewrite needs throughout.

## Open research questions — actually investigate these

1. **Is the QMT formalism's continuity criterion actually well-posed for the software-agent case, and does it generalize meaningfully (even just as a thought experiment) to the human case, or does it break down?** This is the one place real, checkable mathematical/formal-methods work is possible — connect it explicitly to the Identity Architecture monograph's existing treatment rather than re-deriving it from scratch.
2. **What would a Chalmers-style five-question decomposition replace the Composite Identity Fidelity metric with?** For each of the five questions (behavioral resemblance, memory, consciousness, numerical identity, "as good as survival"), what — if anything — could ever be formally measured, versus what is a metaphysical question no measurement resolves? Be explicit about which questions are in-principle answerable by any conceivable technology and which are not.
3. **What is Brillouin microscopy actually validated to measure today, and what is NV-diamond magnetometry actually validated to measure today?** Survey their real, current applications (Brillouin microscopy: mechanical/elastic tissue properties; NV magnetometry: high-sensitivity magnetic field sensing, some neuroscience applications) and state plainly that no established literature connects either to identity, consciousness, or "resonance capture" of a person. This gap should be stated as a finding, not glossed over.
4. **What does the actual current state of connectomics and whole-brain recording technology say about realistic timelines**, per the 2025 state-of-the-field assessment and any more recent work found? Replace the draft's month-scale roadmap with real, sourced, appropriately uncertain estimates.
5. **Is there legitimate, real research on gradual (rather than destructive) replacement models** that would better match Chalmers's continuity-preserving preference, even as pure thought experiment? Survey this and represent it as the philosophically stronger version of the idea, if the paper wants to gesture at "what would be less bad" at all.

## What "legitimate" and "novel" mean here

The defensible thesis for this paper: **TUCR's own formal apparatus (HTLA/HTNLA state representation, QMT continuity criteria) already does something modest and real for software agents; whether or how far that formal apparatus says anything about long-standing open questions in philosophy of mind is a genuinely interesting question worth exploring carefully, with full acknowledgment that the philosophical questions are unresolved and the enabling technology for the human case does not exist and is likely decades away even under optimistic real estimates.** That is a legitimate, citable, honest paper. "Nexus provides a mathematical blueprint for identity transfer" is not, and the rewrite should not gesture toward it even as an aspiration.
