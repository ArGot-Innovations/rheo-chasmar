## Framework 1: Divergent Hypothesis Dynamics — DHD

I like **Divergent Hypothesis Dynamics** because it says exactly what it is without requiring any claim that psychosis itself has predictive power.

Its job is:

> Deliberately create an overcomplete, contradictory, unstable population of possible explanations of the market.

DHD should not choose trades. It should not produce the final forecast. It should not even be required to be internally consistent.

Its output is a structured hypothesis population:

$$
\mathcal H_t
=
\left\{
h_t^{(1)},
h_t^{(2)},
\ldots,
h_t^{(N_t)}
\right\}.
$$

Each \(h_t^{(i)}\) can contain something like

$$
h_t^{(i)}
=
(z_i,\theta_i,c_i,\tau_i,q_i,p_i)
$$

where:

* \(z_i\): proposed latent regime,
* \(\theta_i\): model parameters,
* \(c_i\): causal structure,
* \(\tau_i\): temporal assumptions,
* \(q_i\): predicted observable distribution,
* \(p_i\): provenance/generation history.

The important part is that **DHD is explicitly rewarded for useful diversity rather than immediate correctness**.

A conventional model often implicitly tries to minimize:

$$
\mathcal L_{\text{prediction}}.
$$

DHD instead needs something closer to:

$$
\mathcal J_{\mathrm{DHD}}
=
\alpha N_t
+
\beta D_t
+
\gamma U_t
+
\delta C_t
-
\eta R_t,
$$

where:

$$
D_t
=
\frac{2}{N_t(N_t-1)}
\sum_{i<j}
d(h_i,h_j)
$$

measures hypothesis diversity,

\(U_t\) measures novelty,

\(C_t\) measures coverage of plausible explanatory space,

and \(R_t\) penalizes pure redundancy.

Notice what is **not** in the objective: raw forecast accuracy as the dominant term.

That comes later.

### DHD should intentionally contain several generators

I would not make it one monolithic neural generator. Give it a collection of mathematically distinct operators:

$$
G=
\{
G_{\text{counterfactual}},
G_{\text{causal}},
G_{\text{regime}},
G_{\text{adversarial}},
G_{\text{analogy}},
G_{\text{recursive}},
G_{\text{rare-event}},
G_{\text{inversion}}
\}.
$$

For example, **causal inversion** could deliberately ask:

$$
X\rightarrow Y
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Y\rightarrow X?
$$

Not because that alternative is presumed true, but because the system should actually test it.

Counterfactual generation might construct:

$$
h^{cf}
=
\text{“What market state would explain the evidence if the dominant interpretation were false?”}
$$

Rare-event expansion could deliberately overweight hypotheses having small prior probability:

$$
P(h_i)\ll1,
$$

provided the hypothesis is not logically impossible.

And recursive self-reference could generate hypotheses of the form:

$$
\text{market state}
\rightarrow
\text{agents' beliefs}
\rightarrow
\text{market action}
\rightarrow
\text{new market state}.
$$

That's particularly important for DogeOracle because reflexivity is one of the areas where conventional predictive assumptions become fragile.

### DHD needs controlled instability

The goal shouldn't be maximal chaos.

There should be a divergence-control variable—call it

$$
\rho_t^{D}
$$

for now—governing cognitive expansion.

Low:

$$
\rho_t^D\approx0
$$

means hypotheses cluster tightly around conventional inference.

High:

$$
\rho_t^D\approx1
$$

allows large departures.

But unlike old \(\lambda\), **\(\rho^D\) is an actuator**, not a description of rationality.

That distinction is important.

PDC can say:

$$
\rho_{t+1}^{D}
=
\pi_{\mathrm{PDC}}(S_t),
$$

meaning:

> given the current cognitive state, how radically should we expand the hypothesis space?

Now we've preserved one of the coolest things the original PDC was trying to do without giving a mystical causal role to \(\lambda\).

# Framework 2: Epistemic Adjudication and Calibration — EAC

Then I would create the opposite framework:

## **Epistemic Adjudication and Calibration — EAC**

Its purpose is:

> Determine which products of divergent cognition actually deserve belief.

DHD gets to be imaginative.

EAC does **not**.

EAC should be almost painfully conservative.

For every hypothesis

$$
h_i\in\mathcal H_t,
$$

EAC constructs a score vector rather than immediately compressing everything into one scalar:

$$
\mathbf s_i
=
(
L_i,
C_i,
P_i,
T_i,
O_i,
F_i,
S_i
).
$$

For example:

$$
L_i = \text{predictive likelihood},
$$

$$
C_i = \text{calibration},
$$

$$
P_i = \text{provenance quality},
$$

$$
T_i = \text{temporal consistency},
$$

$$
O_i = \text{out-of-sample performance},
$$

$$
F_i = \text{falsifiability/evidence support},
$$

$$
S_i = \text{stability under perturbation}.
$$

Only after preserving the vector should we construct an aggregate decision statistic if we need one:

$$
A_i
=
w^\top \mathbf s_i.
$$

And even that aggregate should probably be regime-dependent:

$$
w=w(r_t).
$$

Because something like short-horizon order-book prediction and a one-week regime hypothesis should not be scored identically.

## The most important EAC rule

EAC should distinguish:

$$
\text{interesting}
\neq
\text{supported}.
$$

That's critical for this entire project.

DHD might generate an extraordinarily novel hypothesis. EAC can retain it as cognitively interesting while assigning:

$$
P(h_i\mid D_t)\approx0.
$$

That lets DogeOracle remember bizarre possibilities **without believing them**.

You could therefore have at least three states:

$$
h_i
\in
\{
\text{active},
\text{speculative},
\text{rejected}
\}.
$$

I would probably add a fourth:

$$
\text{dormant}.
$$

A dormant hypothesis was unsupported *now*, but not falsified.

That becomes extremely interesting longitudinally.

Suppose DHD generates some strange hypothesis at \(t\), EAC gives it essentially zero weight, and six hours later evidence appears that strongly supports it.

Now you have a measurable phenomenon:

$$
\Delta t_i
=
t_{\text{validation}}
-
t_{\text{generation}}.
$$

That lets us test one of the original intuitions behind DogeOracle:

> Does high-divergence cognition sometimes generate valid hypotheses **before** conventional inference would generate them?

That is vastly more scientifically interesting than simply saying it “predicted the future.”

# Where PDC sits

This is the part I like most.

PDC should not be replaced by either framework.

PDC becomes the mediator:

$$
\boxed{
\text{DHD}
\leftrightarrow
\text{PDC}
\leftrightarrow
\text{EAC}
}
$$

DHD tells PDC:

> Here is everything I can imagine.

EAC tells PDC:

> Here is what survived contact with reality.

PDC decides:

> How should cognition change next?

For example:

$$
u_t^{PDC}
=
(
\rho_t^D,
N_t,
B_t,
\chi_t,
a_t
),
$$

where PDC controls:

* divergence intensity \(\rho_t^D\),
* hypothesis population \(N_t\),
* compute budget \(B_t\),
* evidence-acquisition policy \(\chi_t\),
* active cognitive operator \(a_t\).

Now PDC's Bellman-style evidence acquisition machinery suddenly has an extremely natural job.

For hypothesis \(h_i\):

$$
V_t(h_i)
=
\max
\left\{
V_{\text{stop}}(h_i),
-
c_{\text{sample}}
+
\mathbb E[V_{t+1}(h_i)]
\right\}.
$$

PDC can therefore decide whether an apparently bizarre hypothesis deserves:

* immediate rejection,
* preservation,
* more evidence,
* mutation,
* counterfactual expansion,
* adversarial testing.

That is **much closer to what I think PDC always wanted to be**.

# The really interesting emergent quantity

Once these are separated, we can finally define the phenomenon we're after.

Let cognitive divergence be

$$
D_t.
$$

Let external predictive quality be

$$
Q_t.
$$

Then DogeOracle's central experimental question becomes:

$$
Q_t=f(D_t).
$$

I doubt \(f\) is monotone.

My expectation would be something like:

$$
\frac{dQ}{dD}>0
$$

for a meaningful interval, followed eventually by

$$
\frac{dQ}{dD}<0.
$$

In other words:

$$
\text{too little divergence}
\rightarrow
\text{conventional blindness},
$$

$$
\text{useful divergence}
\rightarrow
\text{novel predictive hypotheses},
$$

$$
\text{excessive divergence}
\rightarrow
\text{epistemic collapse}.
$$

The interesting object is therefore not some universal “psychotic threshold.”

It may be a **regime-dependent optimal cognitive divergence**

$$
D_t^*
=
\arg\max_D
\mathbb E[
Q_{t+\Delta}
\mid
S_t,D
].
$$

Now that's something PDC could actually learn.

And here's where it becomes wonderfully circular in the *good* sense:

$$
\boxed{
\text{PDC learns how irrational-looking its internal cognition should become
in order to maximize rational external performance.}
}
$$

That is a much stronger research proposition than the original \(\lambda<0.3\) construction.
